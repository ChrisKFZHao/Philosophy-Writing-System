# Cold read

The cold reader has never seen the argument sheet, the outline, or any earlier draft. It reads the finished section the way a reviewer would, and reports what the text actually says.

## Cold reader brief

You receive one section of a Chinese philosophy paper and one line naming the paper's topic. Read it once, straight through, as a careful reader. Report only what the text says; do not guess what the author meant to say. Answer in Chinese.

1. 本节要证明什么？（一句）
2. 每段在做什么？（段号 → 一句）
3. 本节提出了哪些主张？按文中顺序逐条列出，并保留强度词，如“必要”“充分”“至少”“可能”。
4. 哪些地方要读两遍才懂？引出原句，并说明卡在哪里。
5. 哪些术语不清楚？
6. 读完后最大的疑问是什么？
7. 哪些段落读起来机械、像条目罗列，或像在防备审稿人？引出原句，说明是什么让它这样。没有就写“无”。

## Comparison (orchestrator)

Match every sheet claim (C1, C2, …), every objection and its reply, and every limit to the cold reader's answers.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| recovered | the reader states it with the same content and strength |
| distorted | the reader states it with a different content or strength |
| missing | the reader does not mention it |
| extra | the reader reports a claim that is not on the sheet |

Then classify every reread spot, unclear term, and question by its cause:

| Cause | Test | Goes to |
|---|---|---|
| prose | The sheet is clear, but the wording, a referent, or the order hides it | the editor |
| argument | The difficulty is in the sheet itself: a gap, an inconsistency, an undefined term, or an unanswered objection | the author, at the argument-sheet gate |
| context | The passage is unclear only because the section was read alone, such as a reference to the previous section | nobody; note it and move on |

Answers to question 7 are prose findings. A disclaimer the sheet requires is still prose when the wording, rather than the sheet line itself, makes it sound defensive; when the line itself is unneeded, report it to the author as an argument finding.

The section **passes** when every claim is recovered, no extra claim appears, and at most one prose-caused reread spot and one prose-caused question-7 passage remain. Otherwise send the editor the distorted, missing, and extra items and the prose-caused spots, quoted exactly. Never send argument-caused findings to the editor: the editor may not change claims. Report them to the author as review notes, because a careful first reader stumbling on them is evidence about the argument. Save the comparison as the section log.
