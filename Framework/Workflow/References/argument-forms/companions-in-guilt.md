# Companions in Guilt Argument

## Form

### Core Structure

```
[1] The anti-realist objects: moral facts are F
    (where F is some allegedly problematic feature: queer, epistemologically
    inaccessible, causally inert, unnecessary for explanation, etc.)
[2] But mathematical facts / modal facts / epistemic facts / normative facts of
    kind K also share the same feature F
[3] We (rightly) do not reject mathematics / modality / epistemology / K
    (and we should not — these domains are indispensable or obviously legitimate)
[4] If feature F does not justify rejecting domain K, it cannot justify rejecting
    morality, unless there is a morally relevant difference between the two domains
    (parity premise — the burden of proof is on the anti-realist)
∴ [5] The anti-realist challenge fails — moral facts are no worse off than other
    accepted abstract/normative domains
```

The argument is structurally an **analogical parity argument**: it identifies a feature that morality shares with a respected companion domain, argues that we do not reject the companion on the basis of that feature, and concludes that we should not reject morality on that basis either. The dialectical burden then shifts to the anti-realist, who must either accept a more sweeping error theory (embracing the unwelcome consequence) or identify a morally relevant disanalogy.

### Two Main Variants

#### Variant 1: Mathematical Companion (against Queerness)

```
[1] Moral facts are queer — they are abstract, causally inert, and known by
    non-sensory means (Mackie's objection)
[2] Mathematical facts are also abstract, causally inert, and known by non-sensory
    means (and thus equally "queer" by Mackie's criteria)
[3] We do not (and should not) reject mathematical facts as queer
∴ [4] Queerness alone does not justify rejecting moral facts
```

#### Variant 2: Epistemological Companion (against Evolutionary Debunking)

```
[1] Moral beliefs are undermined because they are formed by evolutionary processes
    that do not track moral truth (the debunking objection)
[2] Epistemic beliefs / beliefs about logic / perceptual beliefs are also formed
    by evolutionary processes that may not track the relevant truth
[3] We do not (and should not) reject our epistemic or logical beliefs wholesale
∴ [4] Evolutionary etiology alone does not justify rejecting moral beliefs
```

## Distinctive Features

1. **Dialectical, not probative**: The companions in guilt argument typically does not aim to *prove* moral realism. Instead, it shifts the burden of proof — the anti-realist must either embrace an unwelcome broader skepticism or identify a principled disanalogy.

2. **Domain-relative**: The argument's force depends on which companion domain is selected. Mathematical companions work well against queerness-based objections; epistemic companions work well against debunking objections; aesthetic companions work well against arguments from idiosyncrasy.

3. **The parity premise does the work**: The key premise is that the companion domain genuinely shares the problematic feature F. If the anti-realist can show that the companion domain lacks F (or has F in a less objectionable form), the argument collapses.

4. **Cuneo's normative web extension**: Cuneo (2007) develops a stronger version: moral facts are not merely analogous to epistemic facts — they are *entangled* with them. To reject moral facts, you must reject epistemic facts, but epistemic facts are indispensable for any argument whatsoever, including the anti-realist's own argument.

## Evaluation Criteria / Objections

| Objection | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Disanalogy challenge** | The anti-realist can identify morally relevant differences: moral facts are essentially practical/motivating, while mathematical facts are not; the epistemology of mathematics (proof) is more secure than moral intuition; we have better explanations of moral disagreement than mathematical disagreement. |
| **What if the companion is also suspect?** | If the argument shows that mathematics is equally queer, perhaps we *should* be skeptical of mathematics too (Field's nominalism, fictionalism). The companions in guilt argument only works if the companion domain is genuinely secure. |
| **Slippery slope toward global nihilism** | If every debunking challenge rebounds via companions in guilt, it becomes impossible to debunk anything — but that is too strong. Some domains may indeed survive debunking for independent reasons. |
| **The entanglement version may beg the question** | Cuneo's claim that epistemic norms entail moral norms requires substantive argument — the anti-realist can deny the entailment. If epistemic norms are purely instrumental (means-end reasoning), they do not generate substantive moral obligations. |
| **Moral facts are uniquely contentious** | The depth and persistence of moral disagreement distinguishes morality from mathematics. Mathematical disagreement is about proof, not about the existence of the subject matter. This may be a principled disanalogy. |

| Proponent | Companion Domain | Target Objection |

## Reconstruction Notes

- The argument is best reconstructed as a challenge with the structure: "If you reject X because of F, you must reject Y because of F. You do not reject Y. So either reject Y or abandon your objection to X."
- The **implicit premise** is often a principle of uniformity: `[IP] Like cases should be treated alike — if F is not a defeater for K, then F is not a defeater for morality unless a principled difference can be identified.`
- A full reconstruction should also articulate the anti-realist's best response: identifying the morally relevant disanalogy. The companions in guilt argument is dialectical, not demonstrative — its success depends on whether the anti-realist can meet the challenge.
- Note two distinct argumentative strategies: **parity** (morality is no worse than K) and **entanglement** (morality is bound up with K, so rejecting morality forces rejecting K).
