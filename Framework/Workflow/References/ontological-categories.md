# Ontological Categories: A Taxonomy

## Introduction

Ontological categories provide the most general classification of what exists -- fundamental kinds of being. The project of categorial ontology, traceable to Aristotle's *Categories* through Kant, Husserl, Ryle, and contemporary analytic metaphysics, aims at an exhaustive inventory of ultimate kinds of entity.

This taxonomy supports two philosophical tools:

- **Ontological-commitment analysis** (Quinean): what a theory quantifies over, and what categories those entities fall into.
- **Grounding checks**: whether entities of one category depend on another, and whether a category is fundamental or derivative.

Categories are presented hierarchically with definitions, subcategories, and canonical exemplars. Rivalries (where accepting one category obviates another) are collected in Section 6.

---

## 1. Concrete Particulars

Concrete particulars exist in spacetime, are causally efficacious, and are not instantiable (cannot be predicated of another entity as a property is).

### 1.1 Ordinary Objects

Medium-sized dry goods persisting through time with reasonably determinate identity conditions.

| Subcategory | Definition | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| Artifacts | Created for a purpose | Tables, chairs, hammers |
| Natural inanimate | Non-living material bodies | Mountains, rivers, planets |
| Organisms | Living biological individuals | Oak trees, dogs, humans |
| Mereological sums | Arbitrary fusions (contested) | Trout-turkey (Lewis) |

**Key debate**: Restricted (van Inwagen) vs unrestricted composition (Lewis, Sider); identity vs constitution (statue and clay).

### 1.2 Persons / Selves

Entities with consciousness, rationality, and moral agency. Contested whether a distinct category or reducible.

| View | Description | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Animalism | Persons are human organisms | Olson, Snowdon |
| Psychological continuity | Chains of psychologically connected states | Parfit, Shoemaker |
| Substance dualism | Immaterial substances distinct from bodies | Descartes, Swinburne |
| Constitution view | Constituted by but not identical to organisms | Baker |

### 1.3 Events

Dated, unrepeatable occurrences -- "the explosion," "Socrates' death."

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Davidsonian | Bare particulars individuated by causal relations | Davidson |
| Kimian | Structured complexes: ⟨*o*, *P*, *t*⟩ | Kim |
| Property-exemplification | Exemplifying a property at a time | Kim (later), Bennett |
| Process-first | Derivative abstractions from processes | Whitehead, Rescher |

**Individuation**: coarse-grained (shooting = killing) vs fine-grained (distinct).

### 1.4 Processes

Temporally extended occurrences unfolding through time, resisting reduction to instantaneous states.

| Subcategory | Definition | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| Activities | Homogeneous, no endpoint | Running, breathing |
| Accomplishments | Natural culmination | Building a house |
| Achievements | Punctual terminus | Arriving, winning |
| Dispositions-in-action | Manifesting powers | Dissolving, conducting |

### 1.5 Tropes (Particularized Properties)

Properties as particulars: *this* redness of *this* apple, distinct from a duplicate's identical redness. Non-repeatable.

| Type | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Modifier tropes | Inhere in bearer | Lowe, Martin |
| Module tropes | Transferable, no substratum | Williams, Campbell |
| Bundle theory | Objects are compresent tropes | Campbell, Simons |

**Rivalry with universals**: Trope-only ontology dispenses with universals; resemblance is primitive.

### 1.6 Spacetime Points / Regions

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Substantivalism | Points exist independently as container | Newton, Sklar |
| Relationalism | Nothing beyond relations among bodies | Leibniz, Mach, Barbour |
| Structural realism | Nodes with no intrinsic identity | Ladyman, French |

## 2. Abstract Objects

Entities lacking spatiotemporal location and causal powers; typically necessary existents knowable a priori.

### 2.1 Universals

Repeatable entities wholly present in multiple particulars simultaneously. *Redness* as one entity instantiated by many red things.

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Immanent (Aristotelian) | Exist only in instances; *in re* | Aristotle, Armstrong |
| Transcendent (Platonic) | Exist independently; *ante rem* | Plato, Bealer |
| Trope nominalism | Only tropes; universal-talk paraphrased | Williams, Campbell |
| Class nominalism | Properties are sets of (possible) individuals | Lewis |
| Resemblance nominalism | Properties are resemblance classes | Rodriguez-Pereyra |
| Predicate nominalism | Falling under a predicate | Quine (at times) |

### 2.2 Propositions

Primary truth-bearers, objects of attitudes, semantic values of declaratives.

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Structured (Russellian) | Complexes of objects, properties, relations | Russell, King, Soames |
| Unstructured | Sets of possible worlds | Stalnaker, Lewis |
| Act-types | Types of cognitive acts | Soames, Hanks |
| Primitivism | *Sui generis* abstracta | Bealer, Merricks |

### 2.3 Numbers / Mathematical Objects

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Platonism | Abstract, mind-independent | Godel |
| Logicism | Mathematics reduces to logic | Frege; Wright, Hale |
| Structuralism | Positions in structures | Benacerraf, Shapiro |
| Fictionalism | Useful fiction | Field, Yablo |
| Intuitionism | Mental constructions | Brouwer, Dummett |

### 2.4 Sets / Classes

Extensional collections by members. Proper classes too large in NBG.

| Type | Definition | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| Pure sets | From empty set (iterative hierarchy) | von Neumann ordinals |
| Impure sets | Contain urelements | {Socrates, Plato} |
| Proper classes | Too large | Class of all ordinals |

### 2.5 Types / Kinds

Higher-order entities classifying tokens; abstract patterns, not *in* instances (type-token distinction).

| Type | Definition | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| Natural kinds | Carve at joints | *Homo sapiens*, gold |
| Artifactual kinds | By function | Chair, novel |
| Formal types | By structure | The letter 'A', chess |

### 2.6 Possible Worlds

Complete ways things could have been.

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Modal realism | Concrete, isolated universes | Lewis |
| Ersatzism (linguistic) | Maximal consistent proposition sets | Carnap, Jeffrey |
| Ersatzism (property) | Maximal states of affairs | Plantinga, Stalnaker |
| Combinatorialism | Recombinations of actual elements | Armstrong |
| Fictionalism | Useful fiction | Rosen |

### 2.7 Fictional Characters

"Do not exist concretely yet subjects of true predications" (Sherlock Holmes).

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract artefactualism | Abstract artifacts created by authors | Thomasson, Kripke |
| Meinongianism | *Sosein* without *Sein* | Parsons, Zalta |
| Fictionalism | Pretend-reference | Walton |

### 2.8 Musical Works

Repeatable artworks (one symphony, many performances); type-token structure.

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Platonism | Eternal abstract sound-structures | Kivy, Dodd |
| Creationism | Abstracta brought into existence | Levinson |
| Nominalism | Nothing beyond performances/scores | Goodman, Caplan & Matheson |

## 3. Properties and Relations

Ways things are and stand. Cuts across concrete/abstract: immanent universals may be concrete; tropes are concrete particulars yet also properties.

### 3.1 First-Order vs Higher-Order

First-order properties are predicated of individuals (being red, being human); second-order properties are predicated of first-order properties (being a colour); higher-order properties are predicated of level *n* > 1 (being a determinable).

### 3.2 Intrinsic vs Extrinsic

Intrinsic properties (shape, mass) are had solely in virtue of how the object is. Extrinsic properties (being a widow, being 5 km from a city) are had partly in virtue of relations. **Lewis's combinatorial test**: intrinsic iff it can differ between duplicates.

### 3.3 Essential vs Accidental

Essential properties are those without which the object could not exist (being human for Socrates, atomic number 79 for gold). Accidental properties are had but could be lacked (being snub-nosed for Socrates).

### 3.4 Determinables vs Determinates

Determinables (colour, shape, mass) are had only in more specific form. Determinates (red, square, 5 kg) are specific ways of having the determinable. Determinates of the same determinable are incompatible at the same location/time; determinates entail their determinable.

### 3.5 Natural Kinds as Property Clusters

Homeostatic property cluster (HPC) view: kinds as stable clusters of co-occurring properties maintained by causal mechanisms.

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| HPC theory | Clusters held by causal mechanisms | Boyd |
| Real essentialism | Real essences explain clustering | Kripke, Putnam, Ellis |
| Promiscuous realism | Interest-relative; no privileged division | Dupre, Kitcher |
| Practical kind theory | Individuated by explanatory role | Millikan |

### 3.6 Dispositions / Powers

Properties manifesting under stimulus conditions. Contested whether reducible to categorical properties.

| View | Definition | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Categoricalism | Only qualitative, non-modal properties | Lewis, Armstrong |
| Dispositionalism | Identity exhausted by causal/nomic role | Shoemaker, Bird, Mumford |
| Identity theory | Every property both categorical and dispositional | Martin, Heil |
| Pan-dispositionalism | All fundamental properties are pure powers | Bird, Mumford & Anjum |

## 4. Facts and States of Affairs

### 4.1 Facts as Truthmakers

Worldly entities making true propositions true. Armstrong: a particular *a*'s having universal *F*; fundamental building blocks. Tractarian: combinations of objects; the world is the totality of facts. Truthmaker maximalism (every truth has a truthmaker) is contested for negative existentials; non-maximalists exempt necessary and negative truths.

### 4.2 States of Affairs

Entities that can obtain or fail. An obtaining state of affairs is a fact. Types: atomic (*a*'s being *F*), molecular (conjunctions/disjunctions), general (*Everything*'s being material).

### 4.3 Negative Facts

Negative facts ("no hippo in this room") pose a dilemma: suspect negative constituents if they exist; unexplained truth if they do not.

| View | Description | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Negative facts exist | Absences are genuine constituents | Russell (1918) |
| Totality fact solution | One totality fact + positive facts suffices | Armstrong |
| No negative facts | Positive facts logically exclude negations | Lewis, Dodd |
| Absences as genuine | Holes, shadows as concrete negatives | Martin, Mumford |

### 4.4 Totality Facts

Higher-order: that these are *all* the entities of a kind. Armstrong's truthmakers for negative/general truths without negative facts. Contingent; could have been more or fewer.

## 5. Fundamental Categories (Meta-Level)

Second-order frameworks governing ontological thinking.

### 5.1 The Quinean Method

A theory is committed to entities in the range of its bound variables. "To be is to be the value of a variable." Regiment language into first-order logic; no second-class existence; semantic ascent turns ontology into theory choice.

### 5.2 Carnap's Internal/External Distinction

Internal questions (within a framework) answered by rules and evidence. External questions ("do numbers *really* exist?") are pseudo-questions resolved pragmatically. The ancestor of deflationary meta-ontology.

### 5.3 Fundamental vs Derivative Ontology (Schaffer)

Shift from "what exists" to "what grounds what." On the permissive view, almost everything exists; the question is fundamentality.

| Level | Description | Exemplars |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental | Not grounded further | Cosmos, simple parts, fundamental fields |
| Derivative | Grounded in fundamentals | Tables, persons, holes |
| Grounding | Non-causal dependence: "*p* in virtue of *q*" | Fine, Rosen, Schaffer, Correia |

### 5.4 Category Mistakes (Ryle)

Treating a concept of one logical type as another. Ryle's example: visitor sees colleges, library, fields, then asks "But where is the university?" -- the university is a different category, not another building. Surface grammar disguises logical-type differences. Applications: mind-body problem, abstract-object discourse, process reification.

### 5.5 Formal vs Material Ontology

Formal ontology studies the most general structures of being via formal methods (Husserl's *Logical Investigations*; mereology, topology). Material ontology studies specific categories in a domain (Searle's social ontology, ontology of physics). Formal ontology provides structural constraints; a proposed category must pass formal-coherence assessment first.

## 6. Contested Categories

### 6.1 Holes, Shadows, Surfaces (Casati & Varzi)

Quantified-over entities ("there is a hole") lacking positive material presence.

| Entity | View |
|---|---|
| Holes | Immaterial particulars parasitic on hosts |
| Shadows | Spatiotemporal particulars; events, not substances |
| Surfaces | 2D boundaries; dependent on bearers |

**Contestation**: Eliminativists paraphrase into perforated objects, not hole entities.

### 6.2 Tropes vs Universals as Sole Fundamental Category

| Camp | Thesis | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Trope-only | All properties tropes; objects = trope bundles | Attribute agreement without shared universals |
| Universal-only | All properties universals; objects = universal bundles | Instantiation and individuation problems |
| Dual-category | Both exist; different roles | Two fundamental property categories |

### 6.3 Events vs Substances as Fundamental

| Camp | Thesis | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Substance ontology | Substances first; events are changes | Aristotle, Lowe, Wiggins |
| Event/process ontology | Events/processes first; objects are patterns | Whitehead, Rescher |
| Conventionalism | Choice is merely representational | Bennett, Hacker |

### 6.4 Structural Realism: Relations Without Relata?

Ontic structural realism (OSR): at the fundamental level, only relations/structures exist -- no intrinsic individuals.

| Variant | Description | Proponents |
|---|---|---|
| Eliminativist OSR | Objects eliminated; only structures | French, early Ladyman |
| Moderate OSR | Objects exist but no intrinsic nature | Esfeld, Ainsworth |
| Objection | Structure *of what*? | Chakravartty, Psillos |
