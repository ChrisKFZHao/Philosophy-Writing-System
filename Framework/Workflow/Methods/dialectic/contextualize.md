# Contextualization (method.dialectic.contextualize)

> Execution card. Follow it as the complete procedure. Load `references/contextualize-full.md` only when the card does not settle a live ambiguity.

## Use when
- External dialectical space (targets, rivals, tradition, stakes) is needed, especially before judging premise plausibility, inference, or progress.
- Not for internal objection/reply structure (`method.dialectic.dialectical-map`), intellectual biography, sociology of knowledge, or grand-narrative history.

## Inputs
Required: the argument (article/chapter, reconstruction from `method.argument.argument-reconstruct`, or core claims plus reasoning).
Optional:
- Debate domain (if uncertain, list candidates and ask); historical period (precise bounds); key figures/schools (verify, not assume).
- Scope: narrow (immediate neighbors) or wide (multiple periods/traditions).
- Self-reported context (weigh as evidence, not authority).

## Procedure
Set and justify scope: **contemporaneous** = typically 5-15 years before/after composition; **ancestral** = prior work supplying concepts, distinctions, argument forms; **consequential** = later work whose success/failure depends on it. State it in the report; iterate as a spiral.

**Step 1: Identify the Argument's Explicit and Implicit Targets**
- Named target: claim, textual evidence, vulnerable feature.
- Implicit: dominant unnamed, entailed, taken for granted.
- Distinguish **responding to** vs **preempting**.
- Classify **live position**, **historical position** treated as live, **straw figure**.
- Flag confidence; avoid over-/under-reading.

**Step 2: Reconstruct the Debate Tradition**
- Originating questions: puzzles, paradoxes, intuition conflicts.
- Phases: initial formulation, canonical rival statements, normal work, crisis/revision, state at intervention.
- Per phase: key texts, method assumptions, distinctions, formerly decisive arguments.
- **Dialectical affordances**: moves made, responses offered, foreclosures, openings.
- **Normative trajectory** (what counts as learned, corrected, or progress); does the argument accept, challenge, or correct it? Do not endorse that self-understanding.

**Step 3: Map the Argument's Position Among Rival Positions**
- Map synchronically, at the time of intervention. Classify **immediate opponents**, **allied positions**, **distant opponents**, **orthogonal positions**.
- Per position: core thesis, modal strength (necessity/actuality/possibility), argument forms, vulnerabilities.
- Locate its thesis and modal commitments; note any novel distinction redrawing the landscape.
- New position: why unoccupied, enabling resources, effect on the debate.
- Represent compatibility, inconsistency, entailment, orthogonality; spectrum: dimension and coordinate; decision tree: branches taken/foreclosed.

**Step 4: Trace the Historical Development of Key Distinctions and Concepts**
- Per distinction/concept: origin (when, by whom, against what problem, purpose), refinement/challenge/repurposing, alternatives, adopted variant.
- Blocks anachronism; exposes argumentative freight and excluded alternatives; assess whether deployment suppresses viable alternatives.

**Step 5: Identify Intellectual Ancestors**
- Identify the thinkers, texts, and traditions supplying conceptual resources, argument forms, methodological commitments; specify particular moves, not vague labels.
- Distinguish **acknowledged ancestors**, **discernible influences**, **structural inheritances**.
- Per ancestor: what is taken; what rejected, revised, ignored; name mediating figures where applicable.
- **Demonstrated influence** (textual evidence), **probable influence** (known channels, not independently reinvented), **structural affinity** (resemblance without direct evidence). Similarity does not entail influence.

**Step 6: Assess What Is at Stake**
- **Local stakes**, **field-level stakes**, **architectonic stakes**; not every argument has architectonic stakes.
- Per level: dependency relations, load-bearing vs independently supported.
- Author-claimed vs neutral-observer stakes; note and explain divergence, neither at face value nor dismiss without argument.

**Step 7: Evaluate the Argument's Representation of Its Opponents**
- Strongest version? Uncharitable readings? Relevant distinctions ignored? Commitments misattributed without argument?
- Contextual charity: recover the opponent's illocutionary moves, then judge.

**Step 8: Detect Risks of Anachronism and Context-Collapse**
- Check the argument and this contextualization. **Anachronism risk**: applying distinctions, concepts, or standards unavailable to the author or original audience.
- **Context-collapse risk**: flattening multiple audiences, debates, or traditions into one context.
- **Pseudo-perenniality risk**: treating a historically specific question as timeless.
- **Availability test**: were attributed concepts, distinctions, argument forms available to the author? If not, anachronistic.
- **Anxiety-of-influence test**: do not read the argument as knowing its later reception or inflate its scale.
- Mark retrospective comparison as such.

## Required output
Structured report; all components' work required; sections may blend.
1. **Debate Genealogy.** Explanatory narrative (not a chronicle) from enabling origins to encountered state: turning points, originating questions, canonical formulations, revisionary moments, foreclosed/available moves.
2. **Dialectical Position Map.** Synchronic location: commitments, disagreements, alignment, opposition, re-framing.
3. **Target Identification.** Explicit targets, implicit targets, structural opponents.
4. **Intellectual Ancestry.** Thinkers, texts, traditions, resources; provenance of distinctions, argument forms, method.
5. **Tradition Classification.** MacIntyrean: canonical texts, virtues/vices, internal standards of progress, relation to self-understanding.
6. **Stakes Assessment.** Calibrated local, field-level, architectonic stakes.
7. **Canonical Reference Points.** Shared-vocabulary texts, arguments, distinctions, thought experiments.
8. **Contextual Integrity Assessment.** Diagnostic: opponent accuracy, strongest-version engagement, anachronism/context-collapse/straw-manning exposure; what is right or wrong, and what correction changes.

## Boundary rules
- **Break from all traditions**: name the traditions; state its self-understanding as a break; test genuineness (resources strictly unavailable in the prior framework, which must transform its standards to evaluate); rhetorical and substantive breaks can diverge; a genuine break may open a space not capturable by the existing map. Sub-case **declared anti-philosophy**: name the rejected "philosophy" (a particular tradition's conception, not all inquiry); map any positive project.
- **No settled landscape / highly contemporary**: acknowledge unsettledness; give nearest tradition and recent work; project needed crystallization; flag missing evidence (replies, uptake); calibrate confidence; report provisional. **Preprint-era**: value is anticipatory scaffolding.
- **Disputed context**: no resolution by fiat; state competing interpretations and their mapping effect; where feasible, contextualize under each and say which makes the argument most interesting or defensible. Charity is presumptive, not conclusive; balance against fidelity; do not suppress complicating evidence.
- **Cross-cultural / cross-tradition**: map each tradition separately; identify tension/incommensurability; judge coherence or illicit import; flag misreading risk for readers inside one tradition; make standard asymmetries explicit; privilege neither tradition; fair representation.
- **Historical argument read anachronistically**: original-context reading; later reception; flag anachronistic deployments without condemning them; distinguish **original**, **received**, **appropriated argument**.
- **Self-revision**: separate current from earlier position; identify what drove revision; situate as external or internal; judge genuine advance vs restatement.

## Quality bar
1. **Debate literacy**: canonical texts, turning points, standard moves and objections; do not treat familiar moves as novel or settled ones as decisive.
2. **Positional precision**: rivals distinguishable; modal strength, scope, commitments specified; the argument's own position equally precise.
3. **Historical fidelity**: evidence-backed influence, correct direction, no anachronism.
4. **Dialectical honesty**: no inflated significance, unfair opponents, suppressed counter-evidence; note scholarly controversy and its effect.
5. **Tradition awareness**: correct classification, multiple traditions/tensions, the tradition's own standards.
6. **Utility for evaluation**: what becomes salient, which objections open, which charitable readings become possible.
7. **Reflexive awareness**: interpretive choices, historiographical mode, situatedness.
8. **Counterfactual sensitivity**: what evidence would disconfirm each claim.
