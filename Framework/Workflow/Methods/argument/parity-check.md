# Parity Check (method.argument.parity-check)

> Execution card. Follow it as the complete procedure. Load `references/parity-check-full.md` only when the card does not settle a live ambiguity.

## Use when
- Reconstructed argument extends a commitment from A to B for lack of a relevant difference: companions-in-guilt, marginal-cases, consistency arguments.
- Slippery-slope/sorites chains claim each adjacent pair relevantly similar.
- Not for trivial respects (decline standard analysis), incommensurable domains (flag bridging), or pragmatic aims (note it).

## Inputs
Required: a reconstructed numbered-premise argument stating (a) the claim about A, (b) the A–B similarity claim/presupposition, (c) the licensing principle, (d) the conclusion about B—ideally via method.argument.argument-reconstruct; otherwise analysis cannot proceed. Also the parity statement, quoted or paraphrased, interpretive choices flagged.

Optional: domain context (domain-specific criteria); prior admissions (flag all-vs-some); stated principles; posture (offensive/defensive; burden); audience; argumentative history (resemblance to known arguments).

## Procedure

**1. Extract the Parity Claim**
- Three levels: (a) explicit—quote verbatim; translate figures, flag; (b) presupposed—state the suppressed similarity premise even if implausible; unacknowledged reliance risks begging the question; (c) rhetorically needed—what persuasion requires.
- Restate: "A and B relevantly similar in P (P1…Pn); commitment to C about A commits one to C about B."
- Flag gaps; stated weaker than needed = structural weakness.

**2. Identify the Respect of Comparison**
- State the similarity basis precisely enough to classify each feature as inside, outside-compatible, or incompatible.
- Single property or cluster (primary element?); descriptive/normative/structural; intrinsic/relational (relational may collapse formal into substantive parity).
- Stated vs operative mismatch is a common failure; flag thin/thick equivocation; if contested, record both and their significance; note single- vs multiple-respect parity.

**3. Inventory All Differences**
- Sources: noted/conceded; domain context; any competent participant's distinctions; speculative—not established or ruled out (decisive ones → Step 5).
- Categories: descriptive (e.g., causal profile, ontological category; sentience, autonomy; reliability, safety); normative (independent evaluative status, reasons, consequences); structural (relation to other cases, principles, commitments; theoretical cost).
- Err toward inclusion; explain later irrelevance in Step 4; include extrinsic/relational features; separate acknowledged from unacknowledged-not-ruled-out.

**4. Test Each Difference for Relevance**
- Criterion: D is relevant iff its presence in one but not the other, with plausible principles accepted by both (else the opponent), would justify affirming C for one and denying it for the other.
- (a) State the principle making D relevant; none formulable → likely irrelevant. (b) Status: both / arguer / plausible commitment / opponent; disputed → underdetermined. (c) Interaction: irrelevant alone may matter combined; test the constellation—the gestalt may justify differential treatment; check presupposed atomism.
- Assign *clearly irrelevant* (no bearing—orthogonal or excluded by a shared principle), *potentially relevant but underdetermined* (relevant only if disputed theses hold—not settled by the materials; name them and what each party must establish or concede), or *genuinely relevant* (accepted principles explain the differential treatment); state the conclusion tested; interdependent statuses → note interaction.
- Flag binary-to-scalar equivocation (property shared vs same degree) where scalar parity is needed.

**5. Check for Suppressed Disanalogies**
- Identify unknown differences that would break parity if obtained and that the arguer has not excluded.
- Grade: **independently plausible** (evidence, theory, analogy; not decisive) vs **merely possible** (consistent, not motivated).
- Burden: arguer bears it (revisionary) → merely possible may not resist; opponent bears it (defending status-quo differential treatment) → even merely possible may have force.

**6. Assess Transitivity of Parity**
- Tolerance principle: small adjacent differences do not justify different treatment; threshold principled or arbitrary?
- Accumulation test: differences accumulate into a relevant endpoint difference → tolerance not transitive; chain fails despite sound links.
- Higher-order similarity test: direct endpoint parity makes the chain unnecessary (rhetoric may remain).
- Vague boundaries: flag sorites problems and any presupposed resolution; do not resolve the paradox.
- Scope: test regionally and globally; strong tolerance (never justifies difference) vs weak (typically not; accumulation can matter); most chains need strong—identify required vs established.
- Stopping point: an adjacent-pair difference must be principled, not arbitrary; principled stops break the chain, arbitrary ones relocate the problem.

**7. Evaluate Dialectical Consequences**
- If parity holds: what must the opponent accept—unwelcome consequence (*reductio*) or extension? Does success "boomerang" into further parity claims against the arguer? Is the position tenable or abandoned?
- If parity fails: collapse or repair (narrow respect, qualify conclusion, restrict domain)? Does a particularist-style failure undermine parity domain-wide? Does resistance commit the opponent to a principle they may regret or that invites a further parity challenge? Advanced or stalemated? State the next move.
- Surface the deeper disagreement: which principle/framework is at stake, not only who wins.

**All-step rules**
- Iterate: later findings revise earlier steps.
- Document interpretive choices and alternatives; critical engagement, not algorithm.
- Hedge explicitly; name uncertainty: missing information, theoretical indeterminacy, conceptual ambiguity. Flag vulnerabilities on both sides.
- Asymmetric parity (one direction): principled or rhetorical? Context-sensitive relevance: state the conclusion. Defeasibility: "holds" = nothing available in this dialectical context breaks it, relative to materials supplied.

## Required output
**1. Parity Claim Restatement** — A, B, commitment, transfer principle; faithful, ambiguities resolved.
**2. Respect of Comparison** — dimension; single/cluster; D/N/S; stated vs operative; single-/multiple-respect; contested readings.
**3. Difference Inventory** — inclusive D/N/S catalogue; sources; acknowledged/unacknowledged split.
**4. Relevance Assessment** — each difference exactly one of *clearly irrelevant* / *potentially relevant but underdetermined* / *genuinely relevant*, with principle and interaction notes.
**5. Verdict** — one of *Parity holds* / *Parity fails* / *Parity indeterminate* / *Parity is trivial*, plus rationale; indeterminate names what would settle it; all qualified to materials supplied.
**6. Suppressed Disanalogies** — differences that would break parity if they obtained; independently plausible vs merely possible.
**7. Dialectical Consequences** — consequences if parity holds or fails; repair options, consistency pressures, boomerang risk, next move.

## Boundary rules
- **Trivial parity**: a respect so thin nearly any pair satisfies it ("both are events," "both involve causation in some sense")—formally true, vacuous. Flag and decline the standard analysis: relevance cannot be tested without a substantive respect. Explain the triviality, cite dissimilar cases that satisfy it, state the thicker specification needed (genuinely shared, not widely shared, conclusion-connected). Not a parity argument at all.
- **Incommensurable cases**: differing relevance frameworks may defeat parity by incommensurability, not a specific disanalogy. Flag it, identify needed bridging premises; do not supply them—assess the arguer's bridges.
- **Pragmatic relevance**: relevance is strictly truth/justification-directed; pragmatic/contextual considerations go under Dialectical Consequences and do not fix the verdict. Flag primarily pragmatic arguments (practical *reductio*, political intervention), which may be mischaracterized; identify the truth-directed substructure only.
- **Underdescribed cases**: separate absent-by-design differences (benign) from dialectically significant absences (arguer exploits thin description; "no stated difference" ≠ "no relevant difference"). Catalogue suppressed disanalogies aggressively; verdict tentative; recommend neutral fleshing-out; treat schematic description as a possible liability; no determinate verdict on thin cases.

## Quality bar
1. Inventory complete: a competent interlocutor finds their best objections; suspected-irrelevant items included and explained.
2. Every relevance judgment cites a principle; bare "obviously irrelevant" fails; else construct a plausible candidate and flag it contested.
3. Dialectical fairness: parties' standard, both sides charitable; defeat on an unadduced difference only if independently motivated; standard disagreement is deeper.
4. Domain norms stated and justified; no cross-domain import without argument; cross-domain relevance addressed.
5. Distinguish establishing parity, burden-shifting, and failing to shift; burden-shifting alone still matters.
6. Tripartite classification, not binary; state indeterminacy and its resolution; explain reasonable disagreement.
7. Constructive diagnosis: repairs and repairable/fatal split; opponent's options if holds; end as roadmap.
