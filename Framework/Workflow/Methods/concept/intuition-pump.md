# Intuition Pump Analysis (method.concept.intuition-pump)

> Execution card. Follow it as the complete procedure. Load `references/intuition-pump-full.md` only when the card does not settle a live ambiguity.

## Use when
- A thought experiment, vignette, case, or narrative elicits an intuition used as a premise or dialectical leverage; judge whether it bears its assigned weight.
- Not for bare seemings with no constructed scenario (Boundary rule 5); use `method.concept.thought-experiment` instead.

## Inputs
Required: pump text as presented, with framing/priming commentary (flag altering omissions); the supported argument in premise-conclusion form; deployment context — counterexample, illustration, standalone argument, or cumulative case.
Optional: experimental-philosophy data; mechanism literature; competing analyses; authorial commentary (intended effect); intuition-world correlation data.

## Procedure

**Step 1: Identify the Pump Mechanism.**
- Extract from textual and dialectical context; mark load-bearing vs incidental details; analyze the scenario as presented.
- Classify target faculty: perceptual seemings, conceptual competence, moral sentiment, modal imagination, memory-based recognition, linguistic competence, practical reasoning, mathematical or logical intuition.
- For hybrids, name primary and secondary mechanisms and their interaction. Separate causal driver from post hoc rationalization.

**Step 2: Reconstruct the Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain.**
- A — raw intuitive response: inchoate feeling or inclination, not yet propositional.
- B — propositional judgment: truth-evaluable content verified as supported, not over-interpreted. Flag a vague, qualified intuition converted into a precise, unqualified premise.
- C — argumentative premise: its role, typically via generalization. State whether the particular-to-general inference is licensed and make the gap explicit.
- Check imported content: assumptions the reader must already accept. If required, the pump is question-begging — flag.

**Step 3: Classify the Intuition Type.**
- Assign one or more: categorization, moral, modal, conceptual, epistemic, semantic, mathematical or logical, phenomenological, metaphysical.
- Allow hybrid or contested classifications; use them to select relevant faculties, debunking arguments, studies, framings.

**Step 4: Assess the Pump's Cognitive Transparency.**
- Transparent when (a) driving features are identifiable on reflection, (b) the path to judgment is open to scrutiny, (c) truth-conduciveness is judgeable.
- Opaque when elicited below awareness: implicit associations, affective priming, fluency heuristics, anchoring. A methodological liability, not automatic illegitimacy; force drops when truth-tracking cannot be separated.
- Evaluate feeling of rightness (FOR): artificially high FOR suppresses analysis; distinguish genuine confidence from metacognitive illusion.

**Step 5: Check for Framing, Ordering, and Bias Effects.**
- Examine: ordering effects; affective framing ("murder" vs "killing"); implicit contrast cases (make S* explicit); selective description (identify omissions; test a version including them); rhetorical inevitability ("surely," "obviously," "one cannot help but judge," etc.); anchoring (an initial claim setting a baseline).
- For each, propose a neutral re-description preserving structural core. If the intuition does not survive, force is substantially weakened — a framing artifact.

**Step 6: Evaluate Debunking Susceptibility.**
- Families: evolutionary (selection independent of truth); process (unreliable processes; no evolutionary story); genealogical (cultural or historical origins independent of truth).
- State each applicable argument in premise-conclusion form; evaluate soundness.
- Evolutionary and genealogical: adequate and independent of truth? Distinguish truth-tracking-consistent explanations.
- Process: genuinely implicated, unreliable here, and could a reliable process also generate it?
- Classify: (a) fully debunking — no evidential weight; (b) partially debunking — reduced weight; (c) consistent with truth-tracking; (d) inapplicable — wrong target.

**Step 7: Determine Whether the Intuition Carries the Argumentative Weight.**
- Calibrate burden: a decisive counterexample faces a higher standard than one datum in wide equilibrium.
- Assess what the argument needs, whether delivered, and whether a shortfall is fatal.
- Score: mechanism adequacy (wrong faculty = mismatch), chain integrity, empirical robustness, debunking resistance, framing independence.
- Confidence: high (convergent support), moderate (mixed dimensions; reasonable disagreement), low (conflicting signals, insufficient evidence, too ambiguous).

**Step 8: Assess Dialectical Alternatives If the Intuition Is Rejected.**
- Classify exactly one: Collapse (wholly dependent on a load-bearing, contested intuition); Weakening (other premises work independently; plausible but less supported); Restructuring (reformulate without it; state whether stronger or weaker); Burden-shifting (opponent still owes an explanation; assess whether already discharged, e.g. by debunking); Overdetermination (independently supported; redundant — not worthless).
- Justify by argument structure and pump role.

## Required output
1. **Pump Mechanism Classification.** Targeted faculty, primary/secondary mechanisms, interaction.
2. **Intuition Content.** The proposition meant to compel; distinguish from the psychological state, judgment, and premise.
3. **Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain.** Three-stage reconstruction, implicit enrichment identified, gaps flagged where a stage underdetermines the next.
4. **Argumentative Role.** One of: counterexample to a universal claim; modus ponens inference; source of data for reflective equilibrium; intuition of possibility licensing a modal argument; absurdity generator; intuition of explanation. State implied burden.
5. **Empirical Backing Assessment.** Whether the intuition is (a) widely shared, (b) stable across cultures/demographics, (c) framing-responsive, (d) correlated with verifiable features; flag missing or mixed evidence. Scale: "robustly shared across all sampled populations" / "shared within some populations but not others" / "highly variable and culturally or demographically specific."
6. **Framing and Bias Detection.** Each feature, a neutral re-description, and survival assessment.
7. **Debunking Susceptibility.** Each family in premise-conclusion form, soundness evaluated; outcome: fully debunking, partially debunking, consistent with truth-tracking, or inapplicable.
8. **Dialectical Alternatives.** Applicable outcome among Collapse, Weakening, Restructuring, Burden-shifting, Overdetermination, justified.
9. **Verdict on Dialectical Force.** One of: "dialectically decisive" (strong, non-redundant; survives all challenges), "dialectically weighty but defeasible" (genuine, overridable), "dialectically weak" (marginal, easily outweighed), "dialectically inert" (no work beyond other premises), "dialectically counterproductive" (invites damaging critiques elsewhere). Include confidence.

## Boundary rules
1. **Intuitively compelling but empirically false.** Separate felt compellingness (survives refutation) from evidential value (cannot). Decide whether it is a cognitive illusion owed no deference (Muller-Lyer analogy), or persistence shows the theory incomplete.
2. **Culturally specific intuitions.** Evaluate the expertise position against the no-fault-disagreement position. Variation is not automatic debunking, but the proponent must give a non-question-begging reason for privileging one community; absent it, it weighs only there.
3. **Expert vs. lay intuitions.** Determine whether the pump targets lay or expert response. Needing expert discrimination while eliciting lay intuition is a weakening mismatch; if it targets experts and the expertise defense holds, lay variation may be irrelevant.
4. **"Intuition" label as dismissal.** Distinguish analytic use from evasion. Provenance: intuition is fast, automatic, affectively tinged, resistant to control; considered judgment is slow, deliberative, reasoned. Flag a category mistake when a reasoned judgment is dismissed as "mere intuition"; assess whether any such dismissal is warranted.
5. **Genuinely unanalyzable gut feelings.** With no scenario, mechanism, or engineered elicitation — a bare or "rational intuition" — terminate as "not an intuition pump" and refer to `method.argument.premise-detect` (undefended premise) or `method.metaphysics.conceivability-arg` (modal seeming). Otherwise err toward analysis when the judgment is even partly elicited.
6. Stop if the argumentative embedding cannot be reconstructed; refer to `method.argument.argument-reconstruct` first.

## Quality bar
- Mechanism identified precisely enough to be testable; no vague "moral intuition."
- Every inferential step identified, including implicit enrichment; particular-to-general leaps evaluated separately.
- Sharing/universality claims cited or flagged as unverified armchair speculation; absent evidence is a gap, not consensus.
- Debunking reconstructed charitably and tested for soundness; failures noted with successes.
- At least one alternative framing proposed; distinguish illuminating from manipulative rhetoric.
- Trace downstream consequences: does the argument matter if the pump fails?
- Methodologically neutral; the verdict follows from analysis, is defeasible, and names what new evidence could overturn it.
