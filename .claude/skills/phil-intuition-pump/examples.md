# Intuition Pump Analysis Examples

> This document demonstrates the application of intuition pump analysis across five canonical philosophical thought experiments. Each example shows the complete input-to-output process, with distinct analytical dimensions highlighted.

---

## Example 1: Gettier Cases — Pump Mechanism, Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain, Argumentative Role

### Input

> Gettier (1963, "Is Justified True Belief Knowledge?"). Smith and Jones have both applied for a job. Smith has strong evidence for (i) "Jones will get the job, and Jones has ten coins in his pocket." Smith infers (ii) "The man who will get the job has ten coins in his pocket." Unknown to Smith, he himself will get the job, and he also has ten coins in his pocket. Smith's belief in (ii) is justified (inferred from evidence) and true (the man with the job has ten coins). But does Smith know (ii)?
>
> The argument supported: Justified true belief is insufficient for knowledge. The Gettier case is a counterexample to the universal claim that all cases of JTB are cases of knowledge.

### Output

```
## Intuition Pump Analysis Report

### 1. Pump Mechanism Classification

**Primary mechanism:** Conceptual competence. The pump probes the boundaries of the concept KNOWLEDGE by presenting a case that satisfies the JTB conditions yet strikes the reader as not being knowledge. The pump activates the reader's tacit mastery of the concept and elicits a categorization judgment.

**Secondary mechanism:** Modal imagination. The reader must entertain a counterfactual scenario and track the distinction between what Smith is justified in believing (Jones gets the job) and what is actually true (Smith gets the job).

**How the mechanism operates:** The pump constructs a scenario where the connection between the justifier (evidence about Jones) and the truth-maker (facts about Smith) is coincidental. The reader's conceptual competence detects this disconnection — the concept KNOWLEDGE requires a non-accidental connection between justification and truth that the JTB analysis lacks the vocabulary to articulate.

### 2. Intuition Content

**Elicited intuition:** Smith does not know that the man who will get the job has ten coins in his pocket.

**Distinguished from:**
- *Psychological state:* A feeling of epistemic unease — the belief is true but "lucky" in a way incompatible with knowledge.
- *Propositional judgment:* "This is a case of justified true belief that is not knowledge."
- *Argumentative premise:* "Therefore, justified true belief is not sufficient for knowledge."

### 3. Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain

**Stage A — Raw intuitive response:** The reader experiences epistemic discomfort — a sense that something has gone wrong, that Smith's justification does not connect to the truth in the right way. This is inchoate, not yet propositional.

**Stage B — Propositional judgment:** "In this particular case, Smith has a justified true belief that (ii), but Smith does not know that (ii)."

*Interpretive check:* The raw intuition genuinely supports this judgment. The core mechanism (detection of accidental connection between justifier and truth-maker) maps directly onto the concept KNOWLEDGE's anti-luck requirement. No over-interpretation.

**Stage C — Argumentative premise:** "Therefore, justified true belief is not sufficient for knowledge."

*Generalization check:* A single counterexample is sufficient to falsify a universal sufficiency claim. The inference is licensed — no generalization from particular to universal is needed; what is needed is only that the reader grants the judgment in this particular case.

**Imported content:** None. The pump does not presuppose any particular analysis of knowledge; it works against the JTB analysis using the reader's independent conceptual competence.

### 4. Argumentative Role

**Primary role:** Counterexample to a universal claim. The JTB analysis asserts: for all cases, JTB entails knowledge. The Gettier case presents a case where the antecedent holds but the consequent does not.

**Secondary role:** Source of data for reflective equilibrium. The Gettier intuition is a considered judgment that any adequate theory of knowledge must accommodate.

**Dialectical burden:** The pump must establish both that the case satisfies JTB and that it is not a case of knowledge. Both are independently compelling — Gettier's neutral prose trusts the scenario's structure, not rhetorical amplification, to do the work.

### 5. Empirical Backing Assessment

**Consensus:** Broadly shared across populations. Weinberg, Nichols, and Stich (2001) reported East Asian participants more likely to attribute knowledge; subsequent meta-analyses (Machery et al. 2017) found the variation smaller and less stable than initially reported. Classification: robustly shared across most sampled populations, with minor and contested cross-cultural variation.

**Framing sensitivity:** Low. The intuition is relatively stable across minor re-descriptions. The core mechanism (detection of epistemic luck) appears robust across structurally distinct Gettier-style variants (fake barns, stopped clocks, sheep-shaped rocks).

**Correlation with ground truth:** Not directly testable — there is no independent criterion for what knowledge "really" requires beyond conceptual competence.

### 6. Framing and Bias Detection

**Affective framing:** Minimal. Gettier's prose is notably flat and unadorned, avoiding emotionally charged language. The pump is cognitively transparent — it trusts the scenario's structure rather than its presentation to generate the intuition.

**Rhetorical posture:** Mild. "It is equally clear that Smith does not know" — a modest inevitability phrase, but the intuition survives neutral re-description without it.

**Selective description:** Gettier omits why Smith trusts the company president's testimony. This omission is legitimate pump design — the omitted detail does not neutralize the epistemic luck that drives the intuition.

**Neutral re-description test:** "Smith believes (ii). Smith has good evidence for (ii). (ii) is true, but for reasons unrelated to Smith's evidence. Does Smith know (ii)?" The intuition survives.

**Verdict:** Minimally contaminated by framing effects. The intuition is a response to philosophical structure, not narrative presentation.

### 7. Debunking Susceptibility

**Evolutionary debunking:** Inapplicable. Epistemic evaluation of Gettier cases is not plausibly a product of evolutionary selection pressures.

**Process debunking:** Weak. Conceptual competence is generally reliable within its domain. The question is not whether the process is reliable but whether the concept's boundaries are correctly drawn. If competence consistently classifies Gettier cases as non-knowledge, that is evidence about the concept's structure, not a process failure.

**Genealogical debunking:** Possible but weak. Cross-cultural variation raises the question of whose concept is being analyzed, but this does not debunk the intuition — it indexes the analysis to a specific linguistic community.

**Overall assessment:** The Gettier intuition is robust against debunking. The most serious challenge is cross-cultural variation, which does not debunk but raises a scope question the Gettier project can accommodate.

### 8. Dialectical Alternatives

**Outcome: Partial collapse, then restructuring.** If a single Gettier case is rejected, the literature provides multiple structurally distinct variants (fake barns, stopped clocks) that independently elicit the same intuition, providing convergent support. If all Gettier cases are rejected, the JTB analysis survives this challenge but must be defended on other grounds.

**Overdetermination:** The conclusion that JTB is insufficient is now independently supported by theoretical arguments about anti-luck and safety conditions on knowledge. The Gettier case was historically load-bearing but the conclusion is now overdetermined.

### 9. Verdict on Dialectical Force

**Verdict:** Dialectically decisive. The pump provides strong, non-redundant support for the insufficiency of JTB. It targets conceptual competence, is minimally contaminated by framing, survives plausible debunking challenges, and has been replicated across structurally distinct variants. The pump's success has structured an entire subfield of philosophy.

**Confidence:** High. The Gettier pump is among the most thoroughly vetted intuition pumps in analytic philosophy.
```

## Example 2: Foot's Trolley Problem — Intuition Mechanism, Framing Effects, Variation Across Populations

### Input

> Foot (1967, "The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect"). A runaway trolley is headed toward five people. You can pull a switch to divert it onto a side track with one person, killing the one but saving the five. Is this permissible? In a variant: you are on a footbridge next to a large man. Pushing him onto the track would stop the trolley, saving five but killing him. Is this permissible?
>
> The argument supported (canonical deployment): There is a morally significant difference between the switch case and the footbridge case — a datum that consequentialism struggles to accommodate and that deontological distinctions (doing/allowing, double effect, means principle) are designed to capture.

### Output

```
## Intuition Pump Analysis Report

### 1. Pump Mechanism Classification

**Primary mechanism:** Hybrid — moral sentiment and practical reasoning. The footbridge case activates a visceral aversion to physical violence; the switch case engages consequentialist calculation. Both are then rationalized as principled moral judgments.

**Secondary mechanism:** Conceptual competence. The pump probes the boundaries of PERMISSIBLE by presenting structurally similar cases that elicit divergent judgments.

**Mechanism interaction:** This is the critical feature. The footbridge case triggers an automatic emotional alarm (System 1) that is then rationalized as a principled deontological constraint (System 2). Greene's (2001, 2008) dual-process model diagnoses the anti-pushing intuition as affect-driven rather than principle-driven. The pump's dialectical force depends on the reader's not recognizing that affect, rather than principle, is driving the judgment.

### 2. Intuition Content

**Elicited intuition (switch):** It is permissible to pull the switch, killing one to save five.
**Elicited intuition (footbridge):** It is impermissible to push the large man, even though doing so would save five and kill one.

**Asymmetry claim:** The two cases are structurally equivalent (one dies, five live) but elicit divergent moral intuitions, revealing a principle — the doing-allowing distinction, the doctrine of double effect, or the means principle — that consequentialism allegedly cannot capture.

### 3. Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain

**Stage A — Raw intuitive response (switch):** A sense that pulling the switch is reasonable — "the right thing to do." No affective interference; the calculation is clean.

**Stage A — Raw intuitive response (footbridge):** Visceral aversion — disgust and discomfort at the thought of physically pushing a person. Immediate and automatic, preceding deliberative calculation.

**Stage B — Propositional judgments:** (1) "Pulling the switch is permissible." (2) "Pushing the large man is impermissible."

**Stage C — Argumentative premise:** "There is a morally significant difference between killing and letting die (or between intended and foreseen harm) that consequentialism cannot capture."

*Generalization check:* The gap between "these cases feel different" and "there exists a principled moral distinction" is substantial. The pump makes the cases feel different; it does not demonstrate that the felt difference tracks a morally relevant feature. The generalization requires an additional argument — an account of what the relevant feature is — that the pump does not supply.

### 4. Argumentative Role

**Primary role:** Data generation for reflective equilibrium. The pump generates asymmetric intuitive judgments that moral theories must either accommodate (deontology) or explain away (consequentialism).

**Secondary role:** Modus tollens against consequentialism: if consequentialism is true, both cases should be judged equivalently; they are not; therefore consequentialism is false or incomplete.

### 5. Empirical Backing Assessment

**Consensus across populations:** Highly variable. The switch case elicits broad consensus across cultures. The footbridge case shows significant variation: Western, educated populations show the classic asymmetry; some non-Western populations show weaker or absent asymmetry. Cognitive reflection test scores, disgust sensitivity, and psychopathy measures all predict footbridge judgments — those higher in cognitive reflection and lower in disgust sensitivity are more likely to judge pushing permissible.

**Framing sensitivity:** Extremely high. The footbridge intuition depends critically on narrative details: "pushing," "large man," bodily contact, physical proximity. Re-describing the case to eliminate physical contact (e.g., "pulling a lever that drops the man through a trapdoor") significantly weakens or reverses the intuition. Framing is not incidental — it is constitutive.

**Classification:** The basic asymmetry is robust within Western populations but variable across cultures and individual differences. Highly framing-dependent.

### 6. Framing and Bias Detection

**Affective framing:** "Push" carries strong negative valence implicating personal agency and physical force. Neutral re-description: "cause the man to fall onto the track." Intuition weakens under neutral re-description.

**Implicit contrast:** The reader contrasts "pulling a switch" (impersonal, mechanical) with "pushing a man" (personal, violent). This contrast is assumed, not argued, to track a morally relevant difference.

**Anchoring effects:** The 5:1 ratio anchors the reader, but the footbridge intuition is largely insensitive to the ratio — subjects judge pushing impermissible even at much higher ratios — suggesting affect, not calculation, drives the judgment.

**Verdict on framing:** Heavily framing-dependent. The intuition depends on narrative features (physical contact, personal force) that are not philosophically argued to be morally relevant. The pump manufactures the asymmetry it purports to discover.

### 7. Debunking Susceptibility

**Evolutionary debunking (Greene 2008):** Strong. The footbridge intuition is a byproduct of an evolutionarily ancient emotional alarm triggered by the prospect of physically harming a conspecific. The mechanism evolved to inhibit personally administered violence, not to track moral truth. Argument: (1) the intuition is explained by evolved emotional response to physical harm; (2) there is no reason to think this response tracks moral truth rather than ancestral fitness; (3) therefore, the intuition does not provide evidence for a deontological constraint.

**Process debunking:** Strong. The footbridge intuition is driven by the affect heuristic — making judgments based on emotional valence rather than features relevant to the judgment. The affect heuristic is known to produce systematic errors in structurally similar domains. Negative affect (disgust at pushing) is misattributed as a principled moral judgment.

**Overall assessment:** Substantially debunked. The dual-process explanation (Greene) and the affect-heuristic explanation (Kahneman) converge: the intuition tracks an emotional response triggered by features (physical contact) not plausibly morally relevant.

### 8. Dialectical Alternatives

**Outcome: Weakening.** The trolley asymmetry is the central datum deontological theories are designed to capture. If the footbridge intuition is debunked, the case for a deontological constraint is weakened — but not eliminated, because the doing-allowing distinction and double effect doctrine may receive independent support.

**Burden-shifting:** The debunking explanation (Greene) discharges the explanatory burden — the opponent need not accept the intuition's veridicality once its psychological origins are accounted for.

### 9. Verdict on Dialectical Force

**Verdict:** Dialectically weak. The footbridge pump provides only marginal support for the deontological distinction it defends. The intuition is highly framing-dependent and substantially debunked by dual-process and affect-heuristic explanations. The switch-case intuition is more robust, but the asymmetry — the pump's dialectical payload — cannot survive the debunking of the footbridge intuition.

**Confidence:** High on the debunking diagnosis; moderate on the overall verdict (some philosophers defend the distinction on independent grounds). The trolley problem remains valuable as a diagnostic tool for moral cognition, but as an argument for deontological constraints its force is diminished.
```

## Example 3: Jackson's Mary's Room — Intuition-to-Premise Chain, Debunking Susceptibility

### Input

> Jackson (1982, "Epiphenomenal Qualia"). Mary is a brilliant neuroscientist confined to a black-and-white room since birth. She has never seen color. Despite this, she knows all physical facts about color vision — all neuroscience, physics, and functional roles. One day she is released and sees a red rose. Does Mary learn something new?
>
> The argument supported (Knowledge Argument): (1) Before release, Mary knows all physical facts. (2) Upon seeing red, Mary learns something new — what it is like to see red. (3) Therefore, there are phenomenal facts that are not physical facts. (4) Therefore, physicalism is false.

### Output

```
## Intuition Pump Analysis Report

### 1. Pump Mechanism Classification

**Primary mechanism:** Phenomenological intuition. The pump targets the reader's introspective access to their own color experience. The reader knows what it is like to see red and recognizes that this knowledge cannot be conveyed by reading textbooks — no amount of third-person description substitutes for first-person experience.

**Secondary mechanism:** Modal imagination. The pump requires conceiving of a being with complete physical knowledge but no color experience, and judging that this scenario is coherently conceivable. The intuition that Mary learns something new is partly an intuition that the scenario is genuinely possible.

### 2. Intuition Content

**Elicited intuition:** When Mary sees red for the first time, she learns something new — she learns what it is like to see red. This knowledge is not entailed by, deducible from, or reducible to the complete physical knowledge she possessed in the room.

**The critical ambiguity:** Does Mary learn a new *fact* (propositional knowledge), a new *ability* (ability knowledge), or a new *mode of presentation* of an old fact? The pump's force depends on the reader's interpreting the intuition as supporting the first option, but the raw intuition may support only the weaker claim that Mary's epistemic situation changes in some way.

### 3. Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain

**Stage A — Raw intuitive response:** The reader experiences imaginative resistance — a sense that no amount of textbook knowledge could substitute for seeing red. This is felt as an obviousness: "of course Mary learns something new."

**Stage B — Propositional judgment:** "Mary, despite possessing all physical facts before release, acquires new knowledge upon seeing red."

**Stage C — Argumentative premise:** (1) Before release, Mary knows all physical facts. (2) After release, Mary knows a new fact. (3) Therefore, there are non-physical facts. (4) Physicalism is false.

*Chain integrity check — three critical gaps:*

**Gap 1: Fact vs. ability.** The ability hypothesis (Lewis 1988, Nemirow 1990) argues Mary acquires a new ability — to imagine, recognize, and remember red experiences — rather than new factual knowledge. The raw intuition ("Mary's epistemic situation changes") is compatible with this interpretation.

**Gap 2: New fact vs. old fact under new mode.** The phenomenal concept strategy (Loar 1997, Papineau 2002) argues Mary acquires a new phenomenal concept that refers to a physical property under a different mode of presentation. She knew the fact all along under a physical description but now knows it under a phenomenal description.

**Gap 3: Physicalism and supervenience.** If physicalism is the thesis that all facts supervene on physical facts, phenomenal facts can be non-identical to physical facts yet compatible with physicalism. The pump's target is reductive physicalism, not physicalism simpliciter.

**Imported content:** The pump imports a conception of "physical fact" that excludes the phenomenal — precisely what is at issue. If phenomenal facts are themselves physical facts, Mary did not know all physical facts in the room, and the argument collapses.

### 4. Argumentative Role

**Primary role:** Modus tollens against physicalism — if physicalism is true, complete physical knowledge entails complete knowledge simpliciter; Mary has complete physical knowledge but not complete knowledge simpliciter; therefore physicalism is false.

**Secondary role:** Intuition of possibility — the pump is supposed to establish that Mary's pre-release epistemic situation is conceivable, which some (Chalmers) take to entail metaphysical possibility and thus the falsity of physicalism.

### 5. Empirical Backing Assessment

**Consensus:** The intuition that Mary learns something new is widely shared among philosophers and laypeople. However, interpretation of *what* she learns (facts, abilities, concepts) varies significantly, and the modal structure (conceiving "all physical facts") may exceed ordinary cognitive capacity, making survey responses questionably reliable.

**Framing sensitivity:** Moderate. Emphasizing Mary's predictive powers — if she knows all physical facts, can she predict what red will look like? — tends to weaken the intuition (Dennett's "blue banana" variant). The framing of "all physical facts" as an abstract totality, contrasted with the vivid concreteness of seeing red, is a framing asymmetry that may manufacture the intuition of incommensurability.

**Classification:** Widely shared but with significant contestation over interpretation.

### 6. Framing and Bias Detection

**Implicit contrast:** The pump contrasts "dry textbook knowledge" with "vivid first-person experience." Physical knowledge is left abstract and unvivid; phenomenal experience is concrete and salient. The reader compares an impoverished representation of physical knowledge with a rich representation of phenomenal experience.

**Selective description:** The pump does not specify what "all physical facts" includes. Does it include facts about Mary's own brain states? Indexical facts? Similarity spaces of color experience? The imprecision allows readers to fill in an impoverished set, manufacturing the intuition of incompleteness.

**Rhetorical posture:** "It seems just obvious" and "it is inescapable" — phrases that discourage critical engagement with the contested inferences.

**Neutral re-description:** "Mary knows everything completed physics and neuroscience can say about color vision. She has never seen color. Does she come to know something that cannot be expressed in the language of completed physics?" The intuition survives but the reformulated question makes physicalist responses more salient.

**Verdict:** Substantially framing-dependent. The contrast between vivid phenomenology and abstract physical description may manufacture the intuition of incommensurability.

### 7. Debunking Susceptibility

**Evolutionary debunking:** Inapplicable.

**Process debunking:** Moderate. The modal imagination required — conceiving complete physical knowledge — may exceed ordinary cognitive capacity. The reader's judgment that Mary would learn something new may arise from an imaginative failure (inability to vividly conceive complete physical knowledge) rather than genuine modal insight.

**Genealogical debunking:** Inapplicable.

**Overall assessment:** Not fully debunked but dialectical force is reduced by (a) the ambiguity of what Mary learns, (b) the framing asymmetry, and (c) the imaginative-failure explanation. These considerations do not show the intuition is false but reduce the confidence with which it can be deployed as a premise.

### 8. Dialectical Alternatives

**Outcome: Weakening, not collapse.** The anti-physicalist case does not depend entirely on Mary's room. The conceivability of zombies (Chalmers), the explanatory gap argument (Levine), and the hard problem of consciousness provide independent support. Rejection of the Mary intuition weakens but does not collapse the case against physicalism.

**Burden-shifting:** The persistent compellingness of the Mary intuition requires explanation from physicalists. The ability hypothesis and phenomenal concept strategy have been developed to discharge this burden.

### 9. Verdict on Dialectical Force

**Verdict:** Dialectically weighty but defeasible. The pump provides genuine support for anti-physicalism — the intuition is widely shared and tracks a genuine epistemic asymmetry between physical and phenomenal knowledge. However, its force is limited by the ambiguity of the intuition-to-premise transition (fact vs. ability vs. concept), the framing asymmetry, and the availability of substantive physicalist responses. The pump identifies a genuine explanatory challenge but does not, by itself, refute physicalism.

**Confidence:** Moderate. The debate remains live after four decades with sophisticated positions on both sides.
```

## Example 4: Thomson's Violinist — Intuition Type Classification, Dialectical Role

### Input

> Thomson (1971, "A Defense of Abortion"). You wake up connected via tubes to a famous unconscious violinist. The Society of Music Lovers kidnapped you and plugged his circulatory system into yours — only your kidneys can filter his blood. If you unplug, he dies. If you stay connected nine months, he recovers and you disconnect safely. Are you morally obligated to stay connected?
>
> The argument supported: The right to life does not entail the right to use another person's body, even when that body is the only available means of sustaining life. Therefore, even if the fetus has a right to life (granted for the sake of argument), abortion is not thereby impermissible.

### Output

```
## Intuition Pump Analysis Report

### 1. Pump Mechanism Classification

**Primary mechanism:** Moral sentiment. The pump activates outrage at the violation of bodily autonomy — the reader feels the injustice of being coerced to serve as an involuntary life-support system. This affective response is recruited to support a normative principle about the limits of the right to life.

**Secondary mechanism:** Analogical reasoning. The pump is explicitly an analogy — the reader maps features of the violinist case onto pregnancy and transfers the moral judgment across the analogy.

### 2. Intuition Content

**Elicited intuition:** You are not morally obligated to remain connected to the violinist for nine months. The violinist's right to life does not impose an obligation to sustain his life through nine months of bodily intrusion without consent.

### 3. Intuition Type Classification

**Primary type:** Moral intuition — the pump elicits a judgment about permissibility in a normatively charged scenario.

**Hybrid components:**
- *Categorization intuition:* The pump classifies the kidnappers' action as a rights-violation and the demanded sacrifice as supererogatory.
- *Conceptual intuition:* The pump probes the boundaries of "right to life" — does it include the right to use another's body?
- *Phenomenological intuition:* The pump invites the reader to imagine what forced bodily connection would be like, activating empathy and imaginative projection.
- *Epistemic intuition:* The pump elicits a judgment about what one is justified in believing about the scope of the right to life.

### 4. Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain

**Stage A — Raw intuitive response:** Moral outrage — a sense of violation, unfairness, and the injustice of forced bodily servitude without consent. Automatic and affectively valenced.

**Stage B — Propositional judgment:** "In this scenario, you are not morally obligated to remain connected for nine months, even though disconnection causes death."

**Stage C — Argumentative premise:** "The right to life does not entail the right to use another person's body to sustain that life, even at moderate cost to the other person and when the other person is the only available means."

*Analogical transfer check:* The premise is derived by abstracting the violinist case's morally relevant features into a general principle, then applying it to pregnancy via the claim of moral analogy. The key dialectical question is whether the pregnancy case shares the pump's morally relevant features.

### 5. Argumentative Role

**Primary role:** Modus tollens — if the right to life entails the right to use another's body, then you would be obligated to stay connected; you are not obligated; therefore the right to life does not entail the right to use another's body.

**Dialectical maneuver:** Thomson grants the opponent's central claim (the fetus has a right to life) and argues the conclusion (abortion is impermissible) does not follow. This is internal critique — it does not require contesting the opponent's premise.

### 6. Empirical Backing Assessment

**Consensus:** Widely shared — most subjects judge that staying connected is not obligatory. Systematic cross-cultural survey data is sparse. Pro-life respondents may resist, but the pump's framing (kidnapping, no consent, no prior relationship) is designed to elicit consensus even from those who resist the abortion conclusion.

**Framing sensitivity:** The core intuition survives substantial variations — identity of the connected person, duration, mode of connection. However, the analogical transfer to pregnancy is highly sensitive to whether pregnancy resulted from consensual sex, whether there is a special obligation to offspring, and whether the fetus is a "stranger" in the relevant sense.

**Classification:** Widely shared within target populations; analogical transfer heavily contested.

### 7. Framing and Bias Detection

**Affective framing:** "Kidnapped" rules out consent from the outset. This is legitimate — the pump is designed to test the limits of the right to life specifically where consent is absent.

**Implicit contrast:** The pump contrasts "being forced to sustain a stranger" with "choosing to sustain a stranger." The contrast drives the intuition toward "no obligation." Legitimate if the target case (rape) shares the non-consensual feature; potentially misleading if the target case (consensual pregnancy) does not.

**Selective description:** The violinist is a stranger — no prior relationship, no special obligation, no responsibility for his predicament. In pregnancy, whether a special obligation or partial responsibility exists is contested. The omission may bias the analogical transfer.

**Verdict on framing:** Well-constructed for its dialectical purpose. The pump isolates structural features of the right to life and bodily autonomy. The framing is not illicit — the pump's purpose is to force the opponent to identify a morally relevant difference between the pump case and pregnancy. The question of whether pregnancy shares the pump's features is left for further argument, which is dialectically appropriate.

### 8. Debunking Susceptibility

**Evolutionary debunking:** Weak. The intuition that one is not obligated to sustain a stranger at significant personal cost is not plausibly a byproduct of truth-insensitive selection pressures.

**Process debunking:** Weak. The cognitive processes implicated — moral reasoning about autonomy, fairness, and rights — are not independently known to be unreliable in this domain.

**Genealogical debunking:** Possible but weak. One might argue the autonomy intuition is a culturally specific product of liberal individualism, but this shows cultural variation, not unreliability.

**Overall assessment:** Robust against debunking. The most serious challenge is analogical validity, not debunking.

### 9. Dialectical Alternatives

**Outcome: Weakening (if intuition rejected).** The violinist argument is Thomson's central move. If the reader insists staying connected is obligatory, the argument collapses. But rejection is difficult — the scenario makes the obligation deeply implausible.

**Burden-shifting:** Even for those who reject the abortion conclusion, the pump forces articulation of what feature of pregnancy distinguishes it from the violinist case. This advances the dialectic regardless of whether the opponent grants the intuition.

### 10. Verdict on Dialectical Force

**Verdict:** Dialectically decisive (within restricted scope). The pump provides strong, non-redundant support for the claim that the right to life does not entail the right to use another's body. It is minimally framing-dependent (the "kidnapping" framing is legitimate), robust against debunking, and successfully shifts the dialectical burden to the opponent. The pump does not establish the permissibility of abortion but establishes that the right-to-life argument is incomplete — precisely its intended payload.

**Confidence:** High. Among the most dialectically effective intuition pumps in applied ethics.
```

## Example 5: A Pump That Demonstrably Fails — Framing-Induced Reversal in the Asian Disease Problem

### Input

> Tversky and Kahneman (1981). The U.S. is preparing for an Asian disease expected to kill 600 people. Two programs are proposed.
>
> **Gain frame:** Program A saves 200 people. Program B has a 1/3 chance of saving all 600 and a 2/3 chance of saving no one.
>
> **Loss frame:** Program C results in 400 deaths. Program D has a 1/3 chance of no deaths and a 2/3 chance of 600 deaths.
>
> Programs A and C are extensionally equivalent (200 saved = 400 die). Programs B and D are extensionally equivalent. Yet 72% choose A in the gain frame; 78% choose D in the loss frame. The intuition reverses depending on whether outcomes are framed as gains or losses.
>
> This case is not a traditional philosophical intuition pump but a calibration case. It demonstrates in pure form the framing-induced intuition pattern that the analytical framework is designed to detect in more complex philosophical pumps.

### Output

```
## Intuition Pump Analysis Report

### 1. Pump Mechanism Classification

**Primary mechanism:** Practical reasoning — the pump presents a choice between two programs and elicits a preference.

**How the mechanism is subverted:** The pump is designed with two framings of the same underlying choice. Practical reasoning should yield the same output for extensionally equivalent options. That it does not — that preference reverses when outcomes are described as gains vs. losses — reveals that the intuition is not a deliverance of practical reasoning but an artifact of the description. The cognitive process driving the response (reference-dependent evaluation via prospect theory) operates below conscious awareness and is not the process the subject takes it to be.

### 2. Intuition Content

**Elicited intuition (gain frame):** Program A (200 saved) is preferable to Program B (risky option).
**Elicited intuition (loss frame):** Program D (risky option) is preferable to Program C (400 die).

**The demonstrated failure:** A=C and B=D, so the two intuitions are jointly inconsistent — they cannot both be correct if "preferable" means "the option with the better expected outcome." The intuition tracks the description's framing, not the options' underlying value. The framing induces risk aversion in the gain domain and risk seeking in the loss domain, in accordance with prospect theory's S-shaped value function.

### 3. Intuition-to-Judgment-to-Premise Chain

**Stage A — Raw intuitive response:** In the gain frame, the certainty of saving 200 feels reassuring — a bird in the hand. In the loss frame, 400 certain deaths feels unacceptable, making the gamble attractive. The feeling is immediate, automatic, and accompanied by moderate-to-high feeling of rightness.

**Stage B — Propositional judgment:** Gain frame: "Program A is better." Loss frame: "Program D is better."

**Stage C — Diagnostic significance:** The pump demonstrates that framing affects intuitive judgment even when underlying options are identical. By extension, any intuition pump depending on a particular framing of its scenario is vulnerable to the charge that an alternative framing would reverse the intuition. This provides the calibrating template for the framing-detection protocols in the analytical framework.

### 4. Argumentative Role

**Existence proof.** The pump demonstrates that framing-induced intuition reversal is real and powerful. It licenses the application of framing detection to philosophical pumps, where the effect is typically subtler but structurally analogous.
```
