## Example: Dialectical Tree of Srinivasan's Anger Argument

### Input

Based on the previously reconstructed Srinivasan argument + implicit premise detection results.

### Output

```
## Dialectical Tree Report

### Root Node

#### T: "Anger may be apt, and its counterproductivity does not cancel its value"
- Source: Srinivasan, "Anger"
- Core argument: Anger can be "apt" (driven by a genuine moral violation + proportional + does not necessarily involve a desire for revenge)
- Status: **survived** — after objections, the thesis survives

---

### Objection Layer

#### O1: Counterproductivity Argument — attacks [T's overall conclusion]
- Type: External Challenge
- Description: Anger impedes the victim's rational thought → victims ought to control their anger
- Source: Classical anti-anger tradition (Seneca)
- Status: **responded**

#### O2: Internal-value dependence on an unsound analogy — attacks [ARG-02.P2]
- Type: Logical Objection
- Description: Srinivasan's analogy (anger :: aesthetic experience) does not hold —
         the intrinsic value of aesthetic experience is independent of action, but moral emotions are essentially tied to practical reasons
- Status: **unresponded** (it is not mentioned in the notes whether Srinivasan responds to this objection)

#### O3: Normative conflict ≠ injustice — attacks [ARG-03.P6]
- Type: Distinction Objection
- Description: The existence of a normative conflict does not necessarily constitute "injustice" —
         it may merely be a tragic conflict (an unfortunate trade-off),
         rather than a justice problem requiring correction
- Status: **unresponded**

#### O4: The aptness of collective anger is insufficiently argued — attacks the generalization of [ARG-01.P1]
- Type: Logical Objection
- Description: The argument moves from the aptness of individual anger to the aptness of collective anger in a way that involves a leap
- Status: **responded** (partially)

---

### Response Layer

#### R1: Response to O1 — Strategy: Distinction Defense
- Description: The counterproductivity argument conflates "anger" (the emotion) with "expression of anger" (the action).
         One can oppose inappropriate actions without denying the aptness of the emotion itself
- Status: **surviving**

#### R4: Response to O4 — Strategy: Scope Restriction + Distinction
- Description: The aptness of collective anger is an issue insufficiently explored in the existing literature —
         but it can be inferred that the anger of oppressed groups is apt,
         whereas the anger of privileged groups is typically not
- Status: **weakened** — the argument partially survives but its scope is greatly narrowed

---

### Dialectical Tree (Text Diagram)

```
T (survived)
├── O1: Counterproductivity argument ── attacks T overall
│   └── R1 (surviving) ── Distinction defense: anger vs. expression of anger
├── O2: Analogy unsound ── attacks ARG-02's premise
│   └── [no response found] ── awaiting response
├── O3: Normative conflict ≠ injustice ── attacks ARG-03's premise
│   └── [no response found] ── awaiting response
└── O4: Collective anger leap ── attacks the generalization of argument scope
    └── R4 (weakened) ── Scope restriction + distinction
```

### Overall Assessment

- Dialectical depth: 2 layers (objection → response; no counter-response detected)
- Current status of the thesis: **survived** — two objections successfully responded to, two awaiting response
- Key turning point: R1 (Distinction Defense) — successfully dissolved the strongest counterproductivity objection
- Unresolved disagreements:
  * Validity of the aesthetic analogy (O2)
  * The conceptual origin of "normative conflict as injustice" (O3)
```
