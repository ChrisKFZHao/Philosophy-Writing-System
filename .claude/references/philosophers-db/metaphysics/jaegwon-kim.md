# Kim, Jaegwon (1934--2019)
- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy of mind; metaphysics of mind
- **Major Works**: *Supervenience and Mind* (1993), *Mind in a Physical World* (1998), *Physicalism, or Something Near Enough* (2005), *Essays in the Metaphysics of Mind* (2010)
- **Key Positions**:
  - The causal exclusion argument: mental causation is incompatible with physical causal closure; non-reductive physicalism faces a dilemma between epiphenomenalism and reduction
  - The supervenience argument: if mental properties supervene on physical properties, and physical causes are sufficient, then mental properties are causally excluded
  - Conditional reductionism: mental properties must be functionally reducible to physical properties to secure mental causation, but qualia resist such reduction
  - Mereological supervenience: the properties of wholes supervene on the properties and relations of their parts
  - The multiple realization argument against type physicalism does not establish the autonomy of the special sciences
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The causal exclusion argument (formalized): if a mental event M causes a physical event P*, and P* has a sufficient physical cause P, then M is either identical with P (reduction) or M is causally redundant (excluded); given physical causal closure, non-reductive physicalism entails epiphenomenalism
  - The argument from functional reduction: functional reduction requires identifying the mental property with the property that fills its causal role; if no physical property fills the role, mental causation is lost
  - The argument against non-reductive physicalism: non-reductive physicalism tries to have it both ways -- mental properties distinct from physical properties but still causally efficacious in a physically closed world. Causal exclusion shows this is inconsistent

- **Related**: Davidson (anomalous monism as Kim's primary target; the causal exclusion argument targets token identity without laws); Lewis (supervenience framework and structure of causal relata); Putnam (multiple realization as basis for non-reductive physicalism that Kim challenges); Fodor (special sciences and non-reductive physicalism as alternative to Kim's reductionism)
