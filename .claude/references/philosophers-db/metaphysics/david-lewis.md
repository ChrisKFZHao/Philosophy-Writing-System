# Lewis, David (1941--2001)
- **Tradition**: Analytic metaphysics; modal realism; systematic philosophy
- **Major Works**: *Counterfactuals* (1973), *Convention* (1969), *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986), *Parts of Classes* (1991), *Philosophical Papers* (2 vols., 1983, 1986)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Genuine modal realism: possible worlds are concrete entities of the same kind as the actual world; "actual" is an indexical term, not a mark of ontological privilege
  - Counterpart theory: individuals are world-bound; transworld identity is replaced by the counterpart relation, a similarity relation between individuals in different worlds
  - Humean supervenience: all truths supervene on the spatiotemporal distribution of local, perfectly natural properties instantiated at point-sized entities
  - Unrestricted mereological composition: any collection of objects, no matter how scattered, composes a further object; composition is automatic and unrestricted
  - Best-systems account of laws: laws of nature are the theorems of the deductive system that best balances simplicity and strength in describing the mosaic of particular facts
  - Causation is counterfactual dependence: c causes e iff e would not have occurred had c not occurred
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from theoretical utility for modal realism: possible worlds provide an extraordinarily fruitful framework for analyzing modality, counterfactuals, causation, content, and properties; this utility justifies belief in their existence
  - The argument for unrestricted composition: there is no principled way to draw a line between cases where composition occurs and cases where it does not (argument from vagueness); therefore composition is unrestricted
  - The argument for Humean supervenience over governing conceptions of laws: positing irreducible nomic necessitation between universals violates parsimony and introduces mysterious modal connections

- **Related**: Kripke (opponent on modal metaphysics; the Humphrey objection targets counterpart theory); Armstrong (rival on laws of nature -- Lewis's Humeanism vs. Armstrong's necessitation between universals); Stalnaker (co-creator of possible worlds semantics for conditionals); Quine (Lewis's modal realism as a repudiation of Quine's actualist ontological scruples)
