# Armstrong, David (1926--2014)
- **Tradition**: Australian realism; analytic metaphysics; truthmaker theory
- **Major Works**: *A Materialist Theory of the Mind* (1968), *Universals and Scientific Realism* (2 vols., 1978), *What Is a Law of Nature?* (1983), *A World of States of Affairs* (1997), *Truth and Truthmakers* (2004)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Immanent realism about universals: universals are repeatable entities wholly present in each of their instances; they exist only where instantiated (Aristotelian, not Platonic)
  - States of affairs ontology: the fundamental constituents of reality are states of affairs -- particulars having universals; neither particulars nor universals exist independently
  - The nomic necessitation theory of laws: a law of nature is a relation of necessitation (N) between universals; "All Fs are Gs" is a law if N(F, G) obtains
  - Truthmaker principle: every truth has a truthmaker -- an entity in virtue of which it is true; truth supervenes on being
  - Central-state materialism about mind: mental states are identical to brain states, characterized in topic-neutral, causal-functional terms
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument against resemblance nominalism: resemblance nominalism must posit resemblance relations, but these are themselves universals; an infinite regress ensues unless one accepts at least one universal, undermining the nominalist position
  - The inference-to-the-best-explanation argument for universals: the success of scientific practice (measurement, prediction, explanation) is best explained by mind-independent universals that ground objective similarities
  - The truthmaker argument for states of affairs: truths like "a is F" require truthmakers; neither a alone nor F alone can serve; only the state of affairs a's-being-F can make the truth true

- **Related**: Lewis (rival on properties -- Lewis's class nominalism vs. Armstrong's immanent realism; rival on laws -- Lewis's best-systems vs. Armstrong's nomic necessitation); Plato (Armstrong rejects transcendent universals for Aristotelian immanent universals); Martin (collaborator on the truthmaker program); Smart (fellow Australian materialist on the mind-body identity theory)
