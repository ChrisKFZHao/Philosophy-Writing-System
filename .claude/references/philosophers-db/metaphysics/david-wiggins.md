# Wiggins, David (1933--)
- **Tradition**: Analytic metaphysics; philosophical logic; ethics
- **Major Works**: *Identity and Spatio-Temporal Continuity* (1967), *Sameness and Substance* (1980), *Sameness and Substance Renewed* (2001), *Needs, Values, Truth* (1987), *Ethics: Twelve Lectures on the Philosophy of Morality* (2006)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Sortal essentialism: identity conditions for objects are relative to sortal concepts (substance concepts); to be an object is to fall under some sortal
  - The D-thesis: "a = b" is not a well-formed identity statement unless there is a sortal F such that "a is the same F as b"
  - The conceptualist-realist view of sortals: sortal concepts are mind-dependent in origin but answer to real, objective similarities in the world
  - Individual essence: objects have individual essences -- properties an object must have to be the very object it is
  - Moral properties as response-dependent but objective: analogous to secondary qualities, there is room for objectivity without Platonism
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument for sortal-dependence of identity: identity is always "same something"; without a covering sortal concept, identity questions are indeterminate (the famous example: is the boy the same as the general?)
  - The argument against bare particulars and haecceities: objects are individuated by their sortal-specific identity criteria, not by primitive thisness
  - The argument for a via media between realism and anti-realism about values: moral properties are response-dependent in a way analogous to secondary qualities; they are objective but dependent on human sensibilities
- **Relevance**: `phil-identity-persistence` (sortal-relative identity and persistence conditions), `phil-essence-detect` (sortal essentialism and individual essences -- sortals as the framework for essence), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (sortal concepts and identity criteria), `phil-distinction-track` (sortal-based distinctions among objects), `phil-moral-realism` (response-dependent moral realism)
- **Related**: Aristotle (sortal essentialism draws deeply on Aristotle's metaphysics of substance and essence); Geach (collaborator on relative identity; the D-thesis is jointly associated with Geach); Kripke (Wiggins's individual essences parallel Kripkean essentialism but are grounded in sortals rather than rigid designation); Parfit (Wiggins's rich metaphysics of personal identity stands opposed to Parfit's reductionism)
