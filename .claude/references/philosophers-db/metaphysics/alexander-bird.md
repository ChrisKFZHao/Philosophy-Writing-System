# Bird, Alexander (contemporary)
- **Tradition**: Philosophy of science; metaphysics of science; natural kinds
- **Major Works**: *Nature's Metaphysics: Laws and Properties* (2007), *Philosophy of Science* (1998), *Thomas Kuhn* (2000)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Dispositional essentialism: properties are essentially dispositional; the causal powers of a property constitute its essence
  - Laws as flowing from essences: laws of nature are not contingent regularities or relations between universals; they are consequences of the essential dispositional natures of properties
  - Scientifically informed metaphysics: metaphysics should be constrained by and answerable to the findings of mature scientific theories
  - The metaphysics of science: philosophy of science and metaphysics are continuous enterprises; the best theory of properties, laws, and kinds fits with the deliverances of science
  - Fundamental physical properties (mass, charge, spin) are individuated by their causal-nomological roles, which are essential to them
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from the identity of fundamental properties: fundamental properties discovered by physics are individuated by their causal-nomological roles; these roles are essential -- mass is essentially that which resists acceleration and attracts other masses
  - The argument against categoricalism (Humean and quidditistic views): if properties were purely categorical (non-dispositional) and only contingently associated with causal powers, then worlds could differ only by swapping causal roles of properties -- but such worlds would be empirically indistinguishable and violate the principle that genuine differences should be detectable
  - The argument from the regress against categoricalism: if categorical properties require governing laws to bestow their powers, what grounds those laws? Dispositional essentialism avoids the regress by building powers directly into properties

- **Related**: Kripke (Bird's essentialism is Kripkean in spirit but focused on physical properties rather than semantic reference); Armstrong (Bird rejects Armstrong's categoricalism and nomic necessitation in favor of dispositional essentialism); Cartwright (fellow advocate of powers-based metaphysics of science); Fine (shared essentialist framework but Bird's essences are dispositional while Fine's are constitutive)
