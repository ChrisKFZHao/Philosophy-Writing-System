# Chalmers, David (b. 1966)
- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy of mind; metaphysics of consciousness; philosophy of language
- **Major Works**: *The Conscious Mind* (1996), *The Character of Consciousness* (2010), *Constructing the World* (2012), *Reality+: Virtual Worlds and the Problems of Philosophy* (2022)
- **Key Positions**:
  - The hard problem of consciousness: explaining why and how physical processing is accompanied by subjective experience is fundamentally different from explaining cognitive functions
  - Naturalistic dualism (property dualism): consciousness is a fundamental, irreducible feature of the world, not reducible to or derivable from the physical
  - Two-dimensional semantics: the meaning of an expression can be analyzed along two dimensions -- primary intension (a priori, epistemic) and secondary intension (a posteriori, metaphysical)
  - The conceivability argument: zombies (physically identical to us but lacking consciousness) are conceivable; therefore they are metaphysically possible; therefore consciousness is non-physical
  - Panprotopsychism (later position): fundamental physical entities may have proto-phenomenal properties that, in the right combinations, constitute consciousness
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The zombie argument for property dualism: a world physically identical to ours but lacking all consciousness is conceivable; this conceivability entails metaphysical possibility (given the right constraints); therefore consciousness is a non-physical property
  - The knowledge argument (Mary the color scientist): Mary knows all the physical facts about color vision but learns something new upon seeing red for the first time; therefore there are non-physical facts about consciousness
  - The argument from the structure of phenomenal concepts: phenomenal concepts have a special structure explaining why the explanatory gap arises and why physicalism seems false even to type-B physicalists
- **Relevance**: `phil-conceivability-arg` (zombie argument, Mary, and two-dimensional semantics as the framework for evaluating conceivability inferences), `phil-essence-detect` (consciousness and essential vs. necessary properties of phenomenal states), `phil-modal-arg` (two-dimensional semantics as a modal framework), `phil-thought-experiment` (Mary, zombies, inverted spectrum as canonical thought experiments)
- **Related**: Jackson, Frank (the knowledge argument originated with Jackson, who later recanted; Chalmers continues to defend it); Kripke (Chalmers's modal framework extends Kripke's a posteriori necessity into two dimensions); Dennett (chief opponent; Dennett denies that there is a hard problem); Nagel (the "what it is like" formulation of the problem of consciousness in "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?")
