# Enoch, David (b. 1971)

- **Tradition**: Analytic metaethics; robust moral realism; philosophy of law
- **Major Works**: *Taking Morality Seriously: A Defense of Robust Realism* (2011), "The Epistemological Challenge to Metanormative Realism" (2011)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Robust moral realism: there are objective, non-natural, irreducibly normative moral truths that are independent of anyone's attitudes, desires, or stances
  - The third-factor response to debunking arguments: survival (or some other good) is a "third factor" that both causally explains our moral judgments (via evolution) and is correlated with objective moral truths (because survival is good); this reconciles evolutionary explanation with moral knowledge
  - The indispensability argument for moral realism: normative truths are indispensable to deliberation; since we must deliberate, we are committed (pragmatically and epistemically) to the existence of normative truths
  - The argument from deliberative indispensability: practical deliberation cannot avoid presupposing the existence of normative reasons, making normative realism a pragmatic necessity
  - Moral disagreement does not undermine moral realism any more than persistent philosophical disagreement undermines realism about philosophy
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The third-factor argument: if survival is objectively good, then evolution would select for organisms that track what is good-for-survival, which correlates with what is objectively good. Our moral judgments are caused (via evolution) by factors that track objective goodness, even if indirectly. This defuses the Darwinian dilemma
  - The argument from deliberative indispensability: when we deliberate, we necessarily treat some considerations as reasons; we cannot coherently deliberate while believing there are no reasons. Since we must deliberate, we are rationally committed to realism about reasons
  - The argument for robust realism over quietist alternatives: quietist realists (like Scanlon and Dworkin) claim that normative truths exist but refuse to say what they are; robust realists do the metaphysical work
- **Relevance**: `phil-moral-realism` (robust realism as the strongest realist position), `phil-moral-epistemology` (the third-factor account of how we can have moral knowledge despite evolutionary influences), `phil-normativity` (the indispensability of normative reasons for deliberation), `phil-moral-disagreement` (handling the evolutionary challenge to moral objectivity)
- **Related**: Street (principal opponent; Enoch's third-factor response is the leading realist reply to Street's Darwinian dilemma); Mackie (Enoch accepts Mackie's characterization of objective values but denies this makes them unacceptable); Shafer-Landau (fellow non-naturalist realist with a different approach to the epistemological challenge); Scanlon (quietist realist whom Enoch criticizes for insufficient metaphysical explicitness)
