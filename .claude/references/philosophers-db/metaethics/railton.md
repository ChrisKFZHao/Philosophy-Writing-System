# Railton, Peter (b. 1950)

- **Tradition**: Analytic metaethics; naturalist moral realism
- **Major Works**: "Moral Realism" (1986), "Facts and Values" (1986), *Facts, Values, and Norms* (2003), "Naturalism and Prescriptivity" (1989)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Reductive moral naturalism: moral properties (goodness and rightness) are identical to natural properties, specifically those approved by a fully informed, rational agent
  - The ideal-advisor theory: what is morally good for a person is what that person would want themselves to want if they were fully informed, vividly imaginative, and instrumentally rational
  - Moral realism without metaphysics: moral facts are natural facts about what contributes to human flourishing under idealized epistemic conditions; they are objective but not mysterious
  - Explanatory role of moral facts: moral facts play a role in explaining behavior and social patterns, which provides evidence for their existence on the same grounds as unobservable scientific entities
  - The reduction of moral properties is synthetic, not analytic; it is an empirical discovery account modeled on the reduction of water to H2O
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument for the ideal-advisor analysis: we criticize our actual desires from a more informed perspective; this practice implies a standard of assessment -- what we would want if we knew more. The ideal-advisor analysis makes this standard precise and naturalistically respectable
  - The argument for moral realism from explanatory power: moral facts (e.g., the injustice of a social arrangement) explain social phenomena (e.g., protest, reform); this explanatory role justifies belief in moral facts
  - The argument against non-cognitivist and error-theoretic alternatives: these views cannot account for the role of moral judgments in deliberation, planning, and interpersonal criticism; only a realist account captures the phenomenology of moral life
- **Relevance**: `phil-moral-realism` (reductive naturalist realism as a major position), `phil-moral-psychology` (idealized desire and moral motivation), `phil-normativity` (naturalistic grounding of normativity in informed desire), `phil-moral-semantics` (naturalist semantics for moral terms), `phil-moral-supervenience` (synthetic identity of moral and natural properties)
- **Related**: Brandt (precursor; Brandt's "cognitive psychotherapy" as a method for defining good via idealized desire); Foot (naturalistic virtue ethics with a similar Aristotelian-naturalist spirit); Smith, Michael (rail's ideal-advisor overlaps with Smith's rationalist analysis but differs on normative vs. motivating reasons); Parfit (Railton's naturalism as a target in Parfit's arguments for non-naturalist realism)
