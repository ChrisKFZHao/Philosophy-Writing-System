# Korsgaard, Christine (b. 1952)

- **Tradition**: Kantian ethics; constructivism; philosophy of action
- **Major Works**: *The Sources of Normativity* (1996), *Creating the Kingdom of Ends* (1996), *Self-Constitution: Agency, Identity, and Integrity* (2009), *The Constitution of Agency* (2008)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Kantian constructivism: moral obligations are constructed by the activity of practical reason, not discovered as independent normative facts
  - Constitutivism about normativity: the binding force of moral requirements derives from the constitutive standards of practical agency itself
  - Reflective endorsement: morality's authority is established when we reflect on our practical identities and endorse the moral demands these identities place on us
  - The publicity of reasons: genuine reasons are essentially shareable; private reasons are not genuine reasons at all
  - Morality is grounded in the necessity of self-constitution: to act at all, one must act on a maxim one can will as universal law
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from reflective endorsement: to determine what is normative, we must step back from desires and reflect on whether they provide reasons; we must consider our practical identities and whether they obligate us. If we cannot reflectively endorse a purported obligation, it lacks normative force
  - The argument for the necessity of valuing humanity: practical reason requires that we value our own humanity (rational capacity to set ends); consistency requires valuing humanity in all persons; this yields the categorical imperative
  - The argument from the constitution of agency: agency has constitutive standards (unity, efficacy) that are normative for agents; failing to follow the categorical imperative undermines the unity of the self, dissolving the agent into a mere heap of impulses
- **Relevance**: `phil-normativity` (constitutivism about the source of normative requirements -- the central figure for this skill), `phil-moral-realism` (Kantian constructivism as an alternative to both realism and expressivism), `phil-moral-psychology` (reflective endorsement and practical identity), `phil-argument-reconstruct` (Korsgaard's argument from the constitutive conditions of agency)
- **Related**: Kant (primary source; Korsgaard reinterprets Kant's argument for the categorical imperative in terms of reflective endorsement and agency constitution); Nagel (shared emphasis on the impartiality and publicity of reasons); Parfit (Korsgaard's neo-Kantian constructivism as a target in Parfit's defense of non-naturalist realism); Hume (Korsgaard engages Hume's challenge about whether reason alone can be practical)
