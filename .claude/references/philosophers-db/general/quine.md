# Quine, W.V.O. (1908--2000)

- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; naturalism; pragmatism-influenced empiricism
- **Major Works**: "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" (1951), *Word and Object* (1960), *From a Logical Point of View* (1953), *Ontological Relativity and Other Essays* (1969), *Pursuit of Truth* (1990)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Rejection of the analytic-synthetic distinction: there is no principled boundary between truths of meaning and truths of fact
  - Confirmation holism: statements face the tribunal of experience collectively; the unit of empirical significance is the whole of science (the Duhem-Quine thesis)
  - Indeterminacy of translation: there is no fact of the matter about which translation manual is correct; meaning is indeterminate beyond behavioral dispositions
  - Naturalized epistemology: epistemology should be pursued as empirical psychology, not as a priori first philosophy
  - Ontological commitment: "to be is to be the value of a bound variable" -- a theory is committed to the entities that must exist for its quantified statements to be true
  - Inscrutability of reference: there is no fact of the matter about what our terms refer to beyond the structural role they play in our total theory
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The circularity argument against analyticity: attempts to define "analytic" rely on cognate notions (synonymy, meaning, necessity) that are equally in need of explication
  - The argument for ontological commitment via quantification: existential quantification is the primary vehicle of ontological commitment; predicates do not carry commitment on their own
  - The indeterminacy of translation thought experiment (gavagai): even perfect behavioral evidence underdetermines whether "gavagai" means rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or rabbit-stage
- **Relevance**: `phil-ontological-commitment` (the quantifier criterion as the standard method for reading ontological commitments off a theory -- this is the central figure for this skill), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (undermines the analytic-synthetic distinction that classical conceptual analysis presupposes), `phil-premise-detect` (holism challenges individual-premise assessment), `phil-natural-kind-analyze` (naturalized approach to kinds and their individuation)
- **Related**: Carnap (teacher and target; "Two Dogmas" directly dismantles Carnap's framework); Lewis (Quinean naturalist who accepted ontological commitment but rejected Quine's desert landscapes for lush modal realism); Putnam ("The Meaning of Meaning" extends Quinean indeterminacy into semantic externalism); Davidson (student who pushed Quinean holism further toward the principle of charity and radical interpretation)
