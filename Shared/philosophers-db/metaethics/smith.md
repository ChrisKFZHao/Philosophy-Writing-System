# Smith, Michael (b. 1954)

- **Tradition**: Analytic metaethics; moral realism; moral psychology
- **Major Works**: *The Moral Problem* (1994), *Ethics and the A Priori* (2004), *Consequentialism* (2014, edited volume with substantial contributions)
- **Key Positions**:
  - The moral problem: there is a tension between (1) objectivity of moral judgment (moral judgments express beliefs), (2) practicality of moral judgment (moral judgments motivate), and (3) Humean theory of motivation (belief and desire are distinct existences; beliefs alone do not motivate)
  - The rationalist solution: moral judgments express beliefs about what we would desire to do if we were fully rational; fully rational agents converge in their desires, securing objectivity while respecting Humean psychology
  - Dispositional theory of value: to value something is to believe that one would desire it if one were fully rational
  - Analytic functionalism about moral concepts: the concept of rightness is the concept of that which we would all desire to do under conditions of full rationality
  - The practicality requirement on moral judgment is non-negotiable: a metaethical theory that cannot explain why moral judgments motivate is inadequate
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument for the rationalist solution: the moral problem is solved if we identify rightness with what fully rational agents would converge in desiring. This preserves objectivity (there is a fact about convergence), practicality (the content is a desire), and Humean psychology (motivation requires a desire, but the judgment is about desire, not itself a desire)
  - The fetishism argument against externalism: if moral judgment were a belief about a non-motivating fact and motivation required a separate desire, then the reliably motivated good person would be motivated by a desire to do the right thing -- which treats rightness as a fetish, rather than being moved by the features that make something right
  - The convergence argument: rational agents would converge in their desires because rationality consists in coherence among psychological states; fully coherent agents cannot have conflicting ultimate desires
- **Relevance**: `phil-moral-psychology` (the moral problem and the Humean theory of motivation as a structuring challenge), `phil-moral-realism` (rationalist version of analytic naturalism), `phil-moral-semantics` (analytic functionalism about moral concepts), `phil-normativity` (the connection between reasons and rationality)
- **Related**: Hume (Smith's adoption of the Humean theory of motivation as a constraint on metaethics); Mackie (Smith tries to solve the problem Mackie raises -- how could moral facts be both objective and intrinsically motivating?); Parfit (fellow rationalist who grounds normativity in reasons but draws non-naturalist conclusions); Railton (fellow naturalist moral realist who grounds morality in facts about ideal agency but via a different empirical route)
