# Parfit, Derek (1942--2017)

- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; ethics; personal identity; metaethics
- **Major Works**: *Reasons and Persons* (1984), *On What Matters* (2 vols., 2011; vol. 3, 2017)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Non-naturalist normative realism: there are irreducibly normative, objective reasons that are not natural facts; these reasons are fundamental features of reality
  - The convergence argument: Kantianism, contractualism, and consequentialism converge on the same set of moral principles; moral disagreement is less fundamental than it appears
  - Reductionism about personal identity: personal identity is not what matters in survival; what matters is psychological connectedness and continuity (Relation R) with the right kind of cause
  - The repugnant conclusion and population ethics: total utilitarianism implies that for any population of happy people, a much larger population with lives barely worth living is better -- a conclusion Parfit finds repugnant whose implications he systematically investigates
  - The triple theory: Kant's formula of universal law, Scanlon's contractualist formula, and rule consequentialism converge on the same principles when properly understood
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument for reductionism from fission: if a person's cerebral hemispheres were transplanted into two different bodies, both resulting persons would be psychologically continuous with the original; since identity is one-to-one, neither is identical with the original, but the original does not die -- therefore identity is not what matters
  - The convergence argument (the triple theory): Kant's formula, the contractualist formula, and rule consequentialism converge; the main moral theories are not rivals but mutually supporting approaches to the same truths
  - The argument for non-naturalism over naturalism: naturalist reductions of normativity leave an open question; the irreducibility of the normative domain is a Moorean fact about the nature of reasons
- **Relevance**: `phil-identity-persistence` (the reductionist account of personal identity and fission cases), `phil-thought-experiment` (teletransportation, fission, the repugnant conclusion, the non-identity problem), `phil-moral-realism` (non-naturalist normative realism), `phil-normativity` (the fundamentality of reasons), `phil-argument-reconstruct` (Parfit's methodical, exhaustive argumentation style)
- **Related**: Sidgwick (Parfit self-consciously continues Sidgwick's project in *The Methods of Ethics*); Scanlon (Parfit's contractualism incorporates and extends Scanlon's formula); Korsgaard (target of Parfit's anti-constructivist arguments); Williams (Parfit's reductionism about personal identity is criticized by Williams's integrity-based conception of the self)
