# Jackson, Frank (b. 1943)

- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy of mind; metaethics; metaphysics
- **Major Works**: *From Metaphysics to Ethics* (1998), "Epiphenomenal Qualia" (1982), "What Mary Didn't Know" (1986), *Perception: A Representative Theory* (1977)
- **Key Positions**:
  - The knowledge argument against physicalism: there are non-physical facts about consciousness, shown by Mary learning something new upon seeing color for the first time
  - Moral functionalism / analytic descriptivism: moral terms pick out whatever properties fill the relevant functional roles in mature folk morality; moral truths are a priori entailed by descriptive truths
  - The Canberra Plan: philosophical analysis proceeds by (1) identifying the platitudes surrounding a concept, (2) Ramsifying them into a functional description, and (3) empirically investigating what realizes that role
  - Two-dimensional semantics: distinctions between primary and secondary intensions help resolve conceptual puzzles across philosophy
  - (Later view) The knowledge argument shows we need to expand our conception of the physical; physicalism can accommodate Mary's knowledge as knowledge of an old fact under a new mode of presentation
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The knowledge argument (Mary's Room): Mary knows all physical facts about color vision but has lived in a black-and-white room. When she first sees red, she learns something new -- what it is like to see red. Therefore physicalism is false; there are non-physical facts
  - The argument for moral functionalism via the Canberra Plan: moral terms are defined by the network of platitudes in mature folk morality; empirical investigation reveals which natural properties satisfy these platitudes. This yields naturalistic moral realism without semantic revisionism
  - The argument for the autonomy of conceptual analysis: a priori conceptual analysis (identifying platitudes) and a posteriori empirical investigation (identifying realizers) are distinct and complementary stages of philosophical inquiry
- **Relevance**: `phil-conceivability-arg` (the knowledge argument as a conceivability/possibility argument), `phil-moral-supervenience` (the Canberra Plan and descriptive-to-normative entailment), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (Ramsification and network analysis of concepts), `phil-moral-semantics` (analytic descriptivism as a semantic theory for moral terms), `phil-thought-experiment` (Mary's Room)
- **Related**: Chalmers (Jackson's student; Chalmers extends the knowledge argument into the hard problem and property dualism); Lewis (Jackson's Canberra Plan is a systematization of Lewis's method of defining theoretical terms); Smith, Michael (fellow analytic descriptivist; both use Canberra Plan methodology in metaethics); Kripke (Jackson's two-dimensional semantics refines Kripke's insights about a posteriori necessity)
