# MacIntyre, Alasdair (b. 1929)

- **Tradition**: Aristotelian virtue ethics; Thomism; communitarianism
- **Major Works**: *After Virtue* (1981), *Whose Justice? Which Rationality?* (1988), *Three Rival Versions of Moral Enquiry* (1990), *Dependent Rational Animals* (1999)
- **Key Positions**:
  - The Enlightenment project of justifying morality has failed: modern moral discourse is a collection of incoherent fragments from older, coherent traditions
  - Tradition-constituted rationality: standards of rational justification are internal to traditions of inquiry; there is no tradition-independent, neutral rationality
  - Virtue ethics as practice-based: virtues are acquired human qualities that enable practitioners to achieve the internal goods of practices; practices give content to the virtues
  - The narrative unity of a human life: actions are intelligible only within the narrative context of a whole life that has a telos (end or purpose)
  - A tradition is an historically extended, socially embodied argument about the goods that constitute that tradition; traditions can be rationally compared by their capacity to solve problems and explain the failures of rivals
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from interminable moral disagreement: modern moral debates are irresolvable because participants appeal to incommensurable conceptual fragments from rival traditions (the catastrophe thesis)
  - The argument for the virtue-tradition-practice triad: virtues are defined by their role in practices, which are embedded in traditions, which provide the narrative context for individual lives
  - The Aristotelian-Thomistic argument for natural law: biological teleology provides a grounding for ethics that Enlightenment philosophy abandoned and that can be recovered
- **Relevance**: `phil-moral-disagreement` (disagreement as evidence of conceptual breakdown and tradition incommensurability), `phil-dialectical-map` (tradition-constituted reasoning and competing rational frameworks), `phil-normativity` (the grounding of normative claims in tradition and practice), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (concept analysis as tradition-dependent activity), `phil-charity-check` (charitable interpretation of rival traditions)
- **Related**: Aristotle (primary source for MacIntyre's virtue ethics framework); Aquinas (Thomistic natural law tradition MacIntyre endorses as the most adequate tradition); Nietzsche (MacIntyre's diagnosis of modernity mirrors Nietzsche's but offers an Aristotelian solution rather than a genealogical one); Kuhn (tradition-constituted rationality parallels paradigm-dependence of scientific reasoning)
