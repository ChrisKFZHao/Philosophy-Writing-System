# Plato (428--348 BCE)

- **Tradition**: Ancient Greek philosophy; founder of the Western philosophical tradition; rationalist
- **Major Works**: *Republic* (c. 375 BCE), *Phaedo* (c. 385 BCE), *Meno* (c. 385 BCE), *Theaetetus* (c. 369 BCE), *Parmenides* (c. 370 BCE)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Theory of Forms: abstract, non-spatiotemporal entities constitute the ultimate reality; particulars participate in or imitate Forms
  - Knowledge as justified true belief (articulated in the *Theaetetus*)
  - Recollection theory of learning: all knowledge is innate and recovered through dialectical questioning
  - Tripartite soul: reason, spirit, and appetite; justice in the soul mirrors justice in the city
  - Philosopher-kings as ideal rulers in the kallipolis (the just city)
- **Key Arguments**:
  - Meno's Paradox and the slave-boy demonstration: learning as recollection of innate knowledge rather than acquisition of new information
  - The Allegory of the Cave: epistemic ascent from opinion (doxa) to knowledge (episteme), with the Form of the Good as the ultimate object of understanding
  - The Divided Line: hierarchy of cognitive states from eikasia (imaging) through pistis (belief), dianoia (thought), to noesis (understanding)
