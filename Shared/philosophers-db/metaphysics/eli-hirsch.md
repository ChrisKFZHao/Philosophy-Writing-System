# Hirsch, Eli (b. 1938)
- **Tradition**: Analytic metaphysics; philosophy of language; deflationary meta-ontology
- **Major Works**: *Dividing Reality* (1993), *Quantifier Variance and Realism* (2011), "Physical-Object Ontology, Verbal Disputes, and Common Sense" (2005)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Quantifier variance: different ontological frameworks use different meanings of the existential quantifier; there is no uniquely correct meaning
  - Deflationary meta-ontology: many apparently substantive ontological disputes (over composite objects, temporal parts) are merely verbal; each party speaks a different but equally legitimate ontological language
  - Charity in interpretation: when interpreting ontological disagreements, the principle of charity dictates that we interpret each party as speaking the truth in their own ontological language
  - Common-sense ontology: ordinary language and common sense sanction the existence of tables, chairs, and other medium-sized dry goods; philosophical arguments against them are misguided
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from charity for quantifier variance: when the mereological nihilist says "there are no tables," the most charitable interpretation is that they speak a language in which the quantifier is restricted to simples; the ordinary speaker speaks a language in which the quantifier ranges over composites; both speak truly
  - The argument for the equivalence of ontological frameworks: any ontological framework can be translated into any other with no loss of expressive power; therefore there is no fact of the matter about which is correct
  - The argument from ordinary language: our ordinary linguistic practices fix the truth conditions of existence claims; since these confirm the existence of ordinary objects, revisionary ontologies that deny them are implausible
- **Relevance**: `phil-ontological-commitment` (quantifier variance as a challenge to Quinean realism and the claim that ontological disputes are substantive), `phil-distinction-track` (distinguishing substantive from merely verbal disputes), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (conceptual schemes and ontological frameworks), `phil-charity-check` (charity in interpreting philosophical disagreement)
- **Related**: Carnap (the linguistic framework view as intellectual ancestor of quantifier variance); Sider (principal opponent; Sider argues that only joint-carving quantifiers are metaphysically correct); Quine (the quantifier criterion of ontological commitment -- Hirsch accepts the form but denies that it yields unique answers); Putnam (conceptual relativity and internal realism as precursors to quantifier variance)
