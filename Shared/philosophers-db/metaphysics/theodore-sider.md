# Sider, Theodore (b. 1967)
- **Tradition**: Analytic metaphysics; meta-metaphysics; philosophical logic
- **Major Works**: *Four-Dimensionalism* (2001), *Writing the Book of the World* (2011), *The Tools of Metaphysics and the Metaphysics of Science* (2020), *Logic for Philosophy* (2010)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Four-dimensionalism (perdurantism): objects persist through time by having temporal parts at each moment they exist; they are "spacetime worms"
  - Ontological realism about structure: the world has an objective, mind-independent structure; some concepts "carve nature at the joints" while others do not
  - Quantifier variance as insufficient: Hirsch's quantifier variance fails because there is a fact of the matter about which quantifier meaning is joint-carving (more natural)
  - Purity as a theoretical virtue: theories formulated in more fundamental (joint-carving) terms are better theories, all else equal
  - The argument from temporary intrinsics: an enduring object cannot undergo intrinsic change (e.g., from straight to bent) without contradiction unless it has temporal parts
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from vagueness for four-dimensionalism (the Sider-Lewis argument): if composition were restricted, there would be borderline cases of composition, and thus vague existence; but existence is not vague; therefore composition is unrestricted. Combined with temporal-parts theory, this yields four-dimensionalism
  - The argument from metaphysical fundamentality against quantifier variance: Hirsch's quantifier variant "there exists a mereological sum" does not carve at the joints the way the ontologist's preferred quantifier does; ontological disputes are substantive because nature has a privileged structure
  - The knee argument: if someone says "my knee" pointing to a region without joint-carving boundaries, they have not failed to refer; but the object they refer to is less natural. Naturalness matters for reference and ontology
- **Relevance**: `phil-ontological-commitment` (joint-carving quantifiers and substantive ontology), `phil-identity-persistence` (four-dimensionalism vs. endurantism in the persistence debate), `phil-natural-kind-analyze` (naturalness as the guide to kinds and induction), `phil-distinction-track` (real vs. merely verbal disputes in metaphysics)
- **Related**: Lewis (Sider extends Lewis's doctrine of perfectly natural properties into meta-metaphysics and four-dimensionalism); Hirsch (principal rival on quantifier variance and the substantivity of ontological disputes); Quine (Sider's joint-carving naturalism is a descendant of Quine's ontological commitment methodology); van Inwagen (rival on composition; Sider accepts unrestricted composition while van Inwagen restricts it to lives)
