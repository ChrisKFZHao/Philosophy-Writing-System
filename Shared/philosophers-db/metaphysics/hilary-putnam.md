# Putnam, Hilary (1926--2016)
- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; philosophy of language; philosophy of mind; pragmatism (later period)
- **Major Works**: "The Meaning of 'Meaning'" (1975), *Reason, Truth and History* (1981), *Representation and Reality* (1988), *The Many Faces of Realism* (1987), *Renewing Philosophy* (1992)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Semantic externalism: "meanings just ain't in the head" -- the meaning and reference of terms (especially natural kind terms) are partly determined by the external environment and the division of linguistic labor
  - Twin Earth thought experiment: a physically identical duplicate on a planet where the watery stuff is XYZ (not H2O) does not mean water by "water"; reference depends on the actual environment, not internal psychological states
  - Functional isomorphism (early): mental states are multiply realizable; the same mental state can be realized by different physical substrates sharing the same functional organization
  - Internal realism (later period): truth is idealized rational acceptability; the world does not come pre-divided into objects and properties independent of our conceptual schemes
  - The model-theoretic argument against metaphysical realism: any consistent theory can be mapped onto the world in multiple ways; reference is radically indeterminate without pragmatic constraints
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The Twin Earth argument for semantic externalism: Oscar on Earth and Twin Oscar on Twin Earth are molecule-for-molecule identical, yet "water" in Oscar's mouth refers to H2O while on Twin Earth it refers to XYZ; therefore meaning is not determined by internal psychological states
  - The brains-in-a-vat argument (model-theoretic): the claim "we are brains in a vat" is self-refuting because if we were brains in a vat, our words would refer to vat-world objects, not to real brains and vats; the claim cannot coherently assert what it intends to assert
  - The no-miracles argument for scientific realism (early Putnam): scientific realism is the only philosophy that does not make the success of science a miracle
- **Relevance**: `phil-natural-kind-analyze` (semantic externalism for kind terms via the Twin Earth argument), `phil-conceivability-arg` (Twin Earth as a conceivability argument and brains-in-a-vat), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (the division of linguistic labor and the nature of concepts), `phil-modal-arg` (internal realism's critique of metaphysical necessity), `phil-identity-persistence` (functional isomorphism and multiple realization)
- **Related**: Kripke (independently developed the causal theory of reference and necessary a posteriori truths about kinds); Quine (Putnam's semantic externalism builds on Quine's indeterminacy of translation but rejects meaning holism); Boyd (scientific realism and natural kinds; Putnam and Boyd developed the cluster-kind theory together); Burge (independently arrived at anti-individualism through his arthritis thought experiment)
