# Boyd, Richard (1942--2021)
- **Tradition**: Philosophy of science; metaphysics of natural kinds; scientific realism
- **Major Works**: "Realism, Anti-Foundationalism and the Enthusiasm for Natural Kinds" (1991), "Homeostasis, Species, and Higher Taxa" (1999), "How to Be a Moral Realist" (1988), *The Philosophy of Science* (1991, co-edited)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Homeostatic property cluster (HPC) theory of natural kinds: natural kinds are clusters of properties held together by homeostatic mechanisms -- causal mechanisms that maintain the co-occurrence of the properties
  - Scientific realism: mature scientific theories are approximately true; theoretical terms genuinely refer to mind-independent entities
  - Accommodationist view of natural kinds: natural kinds are not essences fixed by microstructural cores but dynamic clusters stabilized by causal homeostatic mechanisms
  - Moral realism grounded in natural kinds: moral properties are homeostatic clusters of natural and social properties; moral goodness is a natural kind
  - Biological species are paradigm HPC kinds: their properties cluster due to gene flow, common selective pressures, and developmental constraints
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from HPC for accommodating biological species: species do not have essences in the microstructural sense (contra Kripke-Putnam for biology); they are maintained by gene flow, reproductive isolation, and ecological niche pressures -- homeostatic mechanisms
  - The inference-to-the-best-explanation argument for scientific realism: the predictive and explanatory success of science is best explained by the truth or approximate truth of scientific theories
  - The argument from naturalistic moral realism: moral properties are natural homeostatic clusters that play a functional role in coordinating human social life; this allows moral realism without non-natural properties
