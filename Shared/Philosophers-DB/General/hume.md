# Hume, David (1711--1776)

- **Tradition**: Early modern empiricism; Scottish Enlightenment; skepticism; naturalism
- **Major Works**: *A Treatise of Human Nature* (1739--1740), *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (1748), *An Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals* (1751), *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779, posthumous)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Empiricism: all ideas derive from impressions; there is no idea without a prior corresponding impression (the Copy Principle)
  - Problem of induction: inductive inferences lack rational justification; they rest on custom and habit, not demonstrative reasoning
  - Bundle theory of the self: the self is not a persisting substance but a bundle of perceptions in constant flux
  - Compatibilism about free will: liberty is the power to act according to the determinations of the will; determinism is compatible with moral responsibility
  - Sentimentalism about ethics: moral distinctions derive from sentiment, not reason; reason is the slave of the passions
- **Key Arguments**:
  - Causal skepticism: our idea of necessary connection has no impression source in the external world; it arises from the mind's expectation after constant conjunction
  - The is-ought gap: one cannot validly derive normative conclusions from descriptive premises without explanation; this "new relation" requires justification
  - The argument from design (via Philo in the *Dialogues*): the analogy between the universe and human artifacts is too weak to support theism

- **Related**: Kant (awakened from "dogmatic slumber" by Hume; the entire First Critique responds to Humean skepticism); Lewis (Humean supervenience as the contemporary heir of Hume's metaphysics of distinct existences); Ayer (logical positivist appropriation of Hume's Fork as the verification principle); Smith, Michael (Humean theory of motivation as a constraint on metaethical theorizing)
