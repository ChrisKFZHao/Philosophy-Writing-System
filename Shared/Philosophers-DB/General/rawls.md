# Rawls, John (1921--2002)

- **Tradition**: Analytic political philosophy; Kantian contractarianism; liberalism
- **Major Works**: *A Theory of Justice* (1971), *Political Liberalism* (1993), *The Law of Peoples* (1999), *Justice as Fairness: A Restatement* (2001), *Lectures on the History of Moral Philosophy* (2000)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Justice as fairness: principles of justice are those that free and equal rational persons would agree to in a fair initial situation
  - The two principles of justice: (1) equal basic liberties; (2) social and economic inequalities must satisfy fair equality of opportunity and benefit the least advantaged (the difference principle)
  - Reflective equilibrium: justification proceeds by adjusting principles and considered judgments until they cohere; this is the method of moral and political philosophy
  - Political liberalism: in a pluralistic society, principles of justice must be justified by public reason independent of comprehensive doctrines
  - The original position: the choice situation behind a veil of ignorance models the moral constraints of impartiality, fairness, and the separateness of persons
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument for the two principles from the original position: rational parties behind the veil would use maximin reasoning under uncertainty, selecting principles that maximize the worst-case outcome
  - The argument for the difference principle: inequalities are justified only if they work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged members of society
  - The overlapping consensus argument: stability in a democratic society requires that citizens affirm the same political conception from within their diverse comprehensive doctrines
- **Relevance**: `phil-thought-experiment` (the Original Position as a canonical philosophical thought experiment), `phil-argument-reconstruct` (the systematic deductive structure of *A Theory of Justice*), `phil-premise-detect` (the method of reflective equilibrium as a procedure for premise evaluation), `phil-normativity` (the foundations of normative political theory), `phil-charity-check` (reflective equilibrium as a model of charitable interpretation)
- **Related**: Kant (Rawls's constructivism is explicitly Kantian; the Original Position embodies the categorical imperative's universalizability requirement); Nozick (libertarian critic of the difference principle in *Anarchy, State, and Utopia*); Dworkin (fellow liberal egalitarian who engaged extensively with Rawls's framework); Scanlon (contractualist who developed Rawlsian methods into a general moral theory)
