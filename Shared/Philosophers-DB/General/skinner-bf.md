# Skinner, B.F. (1904--1990)

- **Tradition**: Radical behaviorism; experimental psychology
- **Major Works**: *The Behavior of Organisms* (1938), *Science and Human Behavior* (1953), *Verbal Behavior* (1957), *Beyond Freedom and Dignity* (1971), *About Behaviorism* (1974)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Radical behaviorism: behavior is controlled by environmental contingencies of reinforcement, not by inner mental states; mentalistic explanations are pre-scientific and circular
  - Operant conditioning: behavior is shaped and maintained by its consequences; reinforcement increases response frequency; punishment decreases it
  - Determinism about human action: all behavior is lawful and determined by genetic and environmental histories; freedom is the absence of aversive control, not an inner capacity
  - The design of culture: society should be deliberately engineered through reinforcement principles to promote human flourishing
  - Private events are physical events: thoughts and feelings are not non-physical causes but are themselves behaviors subject to the same laws of conditioning
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument against mentalism: explanations that invoke beliefs, desires, or intentions are explanatory fictions that merely redescribe the behavior they purport to explain
  - The argument for operant behavior as the primary unit of analysis: unlike respondent (reflex) behavior, operant behavior is emitted rather than elicited and is controlled by its consequences
  - The argument for cultural design: since behavior is already controlled by environmental contingencies, we should replace accidental control with deliberate planning based on scientific principles
- **Relevance**: `phil-argument-reconstruct` (behavioral functional analysis of argument and persuasion), `phil-moral-psychology` (Skinner's eliminativist challenge to belief-desire psychology and its implications for moral motivation), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (the critique of mentalistic concepts as unscientific and circular), `phil-normativity` (normative recommendations encoded in reinforcement schedules)
- **Related**: Watson (founder of behaviorism; Skinner radicalized Watson's methodological behaviorism into metaphysical behaviorism); Quine (allied naturalist who shared Skinner's suspicion of intensional entities and mentalistic semantics); Chomsky (devastating review of *Verbal Behavior* arguing that behaviorist principles cannot explain the creative, generative character of language); Ryle (philosophical behaviorist who, like Skinner, treated mental-state talk as dispositional rather than referring to inner causes)
