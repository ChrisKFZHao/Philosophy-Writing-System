# Carnap, Rudolf (1891--1970)

- **Tradition**: Logical positivism / logical empiricism; Vienna Circle
- **Major Works**: *The Logical Structure of the World* (1928), *The Logical Syntax of Language* (1934), *Meaning and Necessity* (1947), "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology" (1950)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Verificationism: the meaning of a statement is its method of verification; metaphysical statements are cognitively meaningless pseudo-statements
  - The linguistic framework view of ontology: ontological questions are either trivial (internal to a framework) or meaningless (external); there is no genuine metaphysical dispute about what exists
  - The principle of tolerance: everyone is free to construct their own form of language; the choice between frameworks is pragmatic, not factual
  - Explication as philosophical method: replace vague, pre-theoretic concepts (explicanda) with precise, systematic concepts (explicata)
  - Confirmation rather than verification: scientific hypotheses are confirmed to degrees by evidence, not conclusively verified
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The elimination of metaphysics: traditional metaphysical claims are pseudo-statements that violate logical syntax or contain unverifiable terms
  - Internal vs. external questions: asking "Are there numbers?" externally (outside any framework) is meaningless; internally, within arithmetic, the answer is trivially analytic
  - The Aufbau's constitutional system: all empirical concepts can be rationally reconstructed from a basis of elementary experiences
- **Relevance**: `phil-ontological-commitment` (the linguistic framework view as a deflationary approach to ontology that Quine's criterion responds to), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (explication as a refined form of conceptual analysis), `phil-premise-detect` (distinguishing analytic from synthetic claims), `phil-moral-semantics` (emotivist and non-cognitivist theories influenced by Carnap's verificationism about meaning)
- **Related**: Wittgenstein (early *Tractatus* influenced Carnap but later ordinary-language philosophy diverged sharply); Quine (student at Harvard; "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" dismantles Carnap's analytic-synthetic distinction); Ayer (popularized Carnap's verifiability criterion in English through *Language, Truth and Logic*); Hirsch (quantifier variance as neo-Carnapian deflationary meta-ontology)
