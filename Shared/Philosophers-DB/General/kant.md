# Kant, Immanuel (1724--1804)

- **Tradition**: German idealism; transcendental idealism; Enlightenment philosophy
- **Major Works**: *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781/1787), *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785), *Critique of Practical Reason* (1788), *Critique of Judgment* (1790), *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (1783)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Transcendental idealism: the phenomenal world (appearances) is structured by a priori forms of intuition (space, time) and categories of the understanding (causality, substance); the noumenal world (things-in-themselves) is unknowable
  - The categorical imperative: moral requirements are unconditional commands of pure practical reason; act only on maxims you can will as universal law
  - Deontological ethics: the moral worth of an action lies in its being done from duty, not from inclination or expected consequences
  - Synthetic a priori knowledge: there are truths (in mathematics, pure natural science, and metaphysics) that are both informative and knowable independently of experience
  - Transcendental arguments: arguments that begin from an uncontroversial premise and deduce necessary preconditions
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The Transcendental Deduction: the categories are necessary conditions for the possibility of unified, self-conscious experience
  - The Formula of Humanity: treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always as an end and never merely as a means
  - The Third Antinomy: both transcendental freedom and universal determinism are necessary for reason; resolved by the phenomena/noumena distinction
