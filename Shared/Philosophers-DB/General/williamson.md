# Williamson, Timothy (b. 1955)

- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; knowledge-first epistemology; metaphysics; philosophical logic
- **Major Works**: *Knowledge and Its Limits* (2000), *The Philosophy of Philosophy* (2007), *Modal Logic as Metaphysics* (2013), *Vagueness* (1994), *Tetralogue* (2015)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Knowledge-first epistemology: knowledge is the fundamental epistemic state, not analyzable into belief plus justification plus truth plus an anti-Gettier condition; knowledge is a primitive mental state
  - Evidence is knowledge (E=K): one's total evidence is simply the set of propositions one knows; there is no prior, non-epistemic notion of evidence
  - Epistemic externalism: knowledge does not require the subject to be in a position to know that they know; the KK principle is generally false
  - Anti-exceptionalism about philosophy: philosophy is not discontinuous with natural science; it uses abductive reasoning, model-building, and inference to the best explanation just as science does
  - Metaphysical modality: modal truths are not reducible to linguistic conventions or possible worlds; they are objective and substantive features of reality
  - Epistemicism about vagueness: there are sharp boundaries for vague predicates, but we are irremediably ignorant of where they lie
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The knowledge-first argument against reductive analysis: attempts to analyze knowledge into more basic components fail because they rely on knowledge-level notions like "non-accidental" or "safe" belief
  - The E=K equation: one's total evidence is the totality of what one knows; this simplifies epistemology and avoids the problem of psychological evidence
  - The argument for necessitism: necessarily, everything necessarily exists; contingentism about existence is metaphysically unmotivated and generates paradox
- **Relevance**: `phil-modal-arg` (Williamson's epistemic theory of vagueness and his controversial necessitism), `phil-premise-detect` (E=K as a principle for determining evidential premises), `phil-argument-reconstruct` (anti-exceptionalist methodology: philosophy reconstructs arguments the way science does), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (critique of reductive analysis in favor of knowledge-first approaches)
- **Related**: Gettier (the problem Williamson dissolves rather than solves by rejecting the analysis project entirely); Kripke (modal realism as a competitor to Williamson's necessitism); Lewis (modal and ontological frameworks that Williamson systematically engages); Unger (precursor to knowledge-first thinking in skepticism debates)
