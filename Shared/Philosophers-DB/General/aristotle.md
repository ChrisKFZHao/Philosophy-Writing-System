# Aristotle (384--322 BCE)

- **Tradition**: Ancient Greek philosophy; founder of the Peripatetic school; empiricist and systematic philosopher
- **Major Works**: *Nicomachean Ethics* (c. 340 BCE), *Metaphysics* (c. 350 BCE), *Physics* (c. 350 BCE), *De Anima* (c. 350 BCE), *Categories* (c. 350 BCE)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Hylomorphism: substances are composites of matter (hyle) and form (morphe); neither matter alone nor form alone constitutes a complete substance
  - Four causes: material, formal, efficient, and final causes jointly explain why anything is as it is
  - Virtue ethics: eudaimonia (flourishing) as the highest good; virtue (arete) as a mean between extremes of excess and deficiency
  - Essentialism about natural kinds: species have real essences that ground their characteristic powers, activities, and developmental patterns
  - Categories of being: substance, quantity, quality, relation, place, time, position, state, action, passion -- substance is the primary category on which others depend
  - Syllogistic logic: valid deductive reasoning proceeds through syllogistic forms classified by figure and mood
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The Function Argument (ergon argument): the good for humans is rational activity of the soul in accordance with virtue over a complete life
  - The Unmoved Mover: a first cause of all motion that itself is unmoved, pure actuality without potentiality, thinking on thinking
  - The Sea-Battle argument: future contingent propositions are neither true nor false now, challenging logical determinism while preserving bivalence for past and present
