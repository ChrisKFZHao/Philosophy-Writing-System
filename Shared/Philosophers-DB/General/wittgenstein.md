# Wittgenstein, Ludwig (1889--1951)

- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; logical atomism (early); ordinary language philosophy (later)
- **Major Works**: *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (1921), *Philosophical Investigations* (1953, posthumous), *The Blue and Brown Books* (1958, posthumous), *On Certainty* (1969, posthumous)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Picture theory of meaning (early): propositions are logical pictures of facts; language mirrors reality through shared logical form
  - The limits of language: what can be said at all can be said clearly; whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent
  - Meaning as use (later): the meaning of a word is its use in the language-game; meaning is not a mental entity accompanying words
  - Rule-following considerations: no rule can determine its own application; following a rule is a practice embedded in a shared form of life, not an interpretation all the way down
  - Private language argument: a language logically private to a single speaker is impossible; meaning requires public criteria of correctness
  - Family resemblance: concepts like "game" are united not by a common essence but by a complex network of overlapping and crisscrossing similarities
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The private language argument: a private linguist lacks any criterion of correctness for applying signs; whatever seems right would be right, making talk of correctness empty
  - The family resemblance argument against essentialism: it is a philosophical prejudice that all things called by a common name must share a common defining feature
  - The rule-following paradox: no course of action is uniquely determined by a rule because every course can be made to accord with the rule given some interpretation
- **Relevance**: `phil-conceptual-analysis` (family resemblance as alternative to classical necessary-and-sufficient-conditions analysis), `phil-distinction-track` (language-games as distinct normative domains with their own criteria), `phil-premise-detect` (clarifying the logical grammar of philosophical claims), `phil-moral-semantics` (rule-following challenges to semantic theories of moral language), `phil-essence-detect` (anti-essentialist challenge to the whole project of detecting essences)
- **Related**: Russell (early mentor; Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* developed within Russell's logical atomism); Carnap (Vienna Circle member deeply influenced by the *Tractatus* but rejected its mysticism); Kripke (Kripkenstein -- controversial interpretation of the rule-following paradox as a skeptical challenge to meaning); Anscombe (student, close collaborator, translator, and interpreter)
