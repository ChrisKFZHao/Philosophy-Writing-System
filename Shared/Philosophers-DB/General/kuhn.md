# Kuhn, Thomas (1922--1996)

- **Tradition**: Philosophy of science; historical turn in philosophy of science
- **Major Works**: *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* (1962), *The Copernican Revolution* (1957), *The Essential Tension* (1977), *Black-Body Theory and the Quantum Discontinuity* (1978)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Paradigm theory of science: normal science operates within a paradigm -- a constellation of shared commitments, exemplary problem-solutions, and methodological standards
  - Incommensurability: competing paradigms lack a common measure; there is no theory-neutral observation language for adjudicating paradigm disputes
  - Scientific revolutions: science does not progress by gradual accumulation but by revolutionary paradigm shifts that transform the scientific worldview
  - Theory-ladenness of observation: what scientists observe is shaped by the paradigm they accept; there is no neutral observation language
  - No algorithm for theory choice: values like simplicity, accuracy, scope, and fruitfulness are shared but interpreted and weighed differently by different scientists
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from the history of science: careful historical study shows that science does not conform to the logical empiricist model of cumulative progress by accretion
  - The Gestalt-switch analogy: paradigm change is like a perceptual switch; after a revolution, scientists work in a different world (metaphorically speaking)
  - The no-algorithm argument: shared values underdetermine theory choice; paradigm selection cannot be reduced to an algorithm
- **Relevance**: `phil-conceptual-analysis` (conceptual schemes and their epistemic constraints), `phil-argument-reconstruct` (identifying paradigm-relative premises), `phil-natural-kind-analyze` (theory-ladenness affects kind individuation and taxonomy), `phil-distinction-track` (incommensurability and conceptual change across theoretical frameworks), `phil-dialectical-map` (paradigms as structured dialectical positions)
- **Related**: Carnap (Kuhn's target in the philosophy of science; *Structure* undermines the unity-of-science program by rejecting cumulative progress); Quine (underdetermination of theory by evidence as a precursor to incommensurability); Feyerabend (radicalized Kuhn's incommensurability thesis into epistemological anarchism); Foucault (both emphasize historical discontinuity in knowledge systems)
