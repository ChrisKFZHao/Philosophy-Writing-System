# Shafer-Landau, Russ (b. 1963)

- **Tradition**: Analytic metaethics; non-naturalist moral realism
- **Major Works**: *Moral Realism: A Defence* (2003), *Whatever Happened to Good and Evil?* (2004), *The Fundamentals of Ethics* (2009), *Oxford Studies in Metaethics* (series editor, 2006--present)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Robust moral realism: moral properties are objective, sui generis, and non-natural; moral truths exist independently of any stance or attitude
  - Moral rationalism: moral facts are intrinsically reason-giving; to judge something wrong is to judge that there is reason not to do it
  - Companions in guilt strategy: moral properties are no more problematic than epistemic or mathematical properties; if one accepts the latter, one should accept the former
  - Self-evidence of fundamental moral truths: basic moral principles are self-evident to the sufficiently mature and attentive agent
  - The supervenience of moral properties on natural properties poses no special problem for non-naturalism; covariance does not entail identity or reduction
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument from companions in guilt: Mackie's argument from queerness would equally condemn epistemic norms, mathematical truths, and logical truths as "queer"; but we accept these. Therefore queerness is not a decisive objection to moral realism
  - The argument for non-naturalism over naturalism: naturalist reductions of moral properties fail the open question test and cannot capture the intrinsic normativity of moral facts
  - The argument from the explanatory role of moral facts: moral facts explain non-moral facts (why slavery was abolished, why actions inspire outrage); this explanatory power justifies belief in moral facts
- **Relevance**: `phil-moral-realism` (robust non-naturalist realism as a central position), `phil-moral-epistemology` (a priori moral knowledge and self-evidence), `phil-moral-supervenience` (compatibility of non-naturalism and supervenience), `phil-moral-semantics` (cognitivist semantics for moral language)
- **Related**: Moore (Shafer-Landau revives a Moorean non-naturalist realism with better defenses against Mackiean and semantic objections); Mackie (principal target; the companions-in-guilt strategy aims to neutralize the argument from queerness); Parfit (fellow non-naturalist normative realist; shared view that normative truths are irreducible); Enoch (fellow robust realist with a distinct approach to the epistemological challenge)
