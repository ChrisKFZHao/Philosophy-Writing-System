# Williams, Bernard (1929--2003)

- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; ethics; personal identity; moral psychology
- **Major Works**: *Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy* (1985), *Moral Luck* (1981), *Shame and Necessity* (1993), *Truth and Truthfulness* (2002), *Problems of the Self* (1973)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Internalism about reasons: an agent has a reason to act only if that reason connects to something in the agent's existing subjective motivational set (desires, projects, commitments)
  - Rejection of the "morality system": the peculiar institution of morality (with its emphasis on obligation, blame, and impartiality) distorts ethical life; we should return to a broader, ancient conception
  - Moral luck: moral assessment depends on factors beyond the agent's control -- constitutive luck (who you are), circumstantial luck (what situations you encounter), and resultant luck (how things turn out)
  - The integrity objection to utilitarianism: utilitarianism alienates agents from their deepest ground projects by requiring them to abandon these whenever utility calculation demands
  - Thick ethical concepts (courage, cruelty, betrayal) are both world-guided and action-guiding; they resist the fact-value distinction
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The argument for reasons internalism: if someone has a reason to do something, they must be able to reach that reason by a sound deliberative route from their existing motivations; otherwise "having a reason" would have no explanatory connection to action
  - The Gauguin argument for moral luck: justification of Gauguin's decision to abandon his family for art depends on his artistic success beyond his control; the moral quality of his life is partly determined by luck
  - The argument against the Archimedean point: there is no standpoint outside our ethical practices from which we can justify them; the demand for external justification is incoherent
- **Relevance**: `phil-normativity` (internalism about reasons and the source of normative force), `phil-moral-psychology` (internalism, moral luck, and the limits of impartial morality), `phil-thought-experiment` (the Gauguin case, Jim and the Indians), `phil-moral-realism` (critique of the "absolute conception" of moral reality)
- **Related**: Hume (Williams's reasons internalism develops Hume's insight that reason serves the passions); Kant (Williams opposes the Kantian impartialist, obligation-centered conception of ethics); Nagel (co-originator of the moral luck problem; Nagel draws different conclusions); MacIntyre (both critique modern moral philosophy and seek resources in ancient ethics)
