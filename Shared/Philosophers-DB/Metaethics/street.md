# Street, Sharon (b. 1973)

- **Tradition**: Analytic metaethics; evolutionary debunking arguments; Humean constructivism
- **Major Works**: "A Darwinian Dilemma for Realist Theories of Value" (2006), "What Is Constructivism in Ethics and Metaethics?" (2010), "Objectivity and Truth: You'd Better Rethink It" (2011), "Coming to Terms with Contingency" (2012)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Humean constructivism: normative truths are constituted by what follows from the practical standpoint of valuing; there are no stance-independent normative truths
  - Evolutionary debunking of moral realism: the evolutionary origins of our moral intuitions undercut their claim to track objective moral truths
  - The Darwinian dilemma: moral realists face a choice between denying that evolution influenced our moral beliefs (implausible) or positing an implausible tracking relation between evolution and moral truth
  - If evolutionary forces thoroughly shaped our evaluative attitudes and there is no reason to think those forces tracked moral truth, then our moral judgments are unjustified
  - Anti-realist metaethics is compatible with full-blooded normative ethics; denying moral realism does not entail nihilism
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The Darwinian Dilemma argument: Premise 1: our moral judgments are deeply shaped by evolutionary pressures selecting for reproductive fitness, not moral truth. Premise 2: moral realists must either (a) accept that evolution tracked moral truth (implausible) or (b) deny that evolution explains our moral beliefs (implausible). Conclusion: moral realism cannot account for the reliability of our moral judgments
  - The argument for Humean constructivism: if all normative truths are constituted by what is entailed by the practical standpoint, the Darwinian dilemma does not arise; our evaluative attitudes are the ultimate source of normativity, not a fallible detector of it
  - The anti-realist argument from the best explanation: the evolutionary explanation of our moral intuitions makes no reference to objective moral facts; by parsimony, we should not posit such facts
- **Relevance**: `phil-moral-epistemology` (evolutionary debunking as a challenge to moral knowledge), `phil-moral-realism` (the Darwinian dilemma as a formidable challenge to all forms of moral realism), `phil-moral-disagreement` (evolutionary debunking as a sophisticated version of the argument from disagreement), `phil-normativity` (Humean constructivism as an account of the source of normativity)
- **Related**: Mackie (Street's evolutionary debunking is a contemporary development of Mackie's argument from queerness and relativity); Hume (Street's constructivism is explicitly Humean; the practical standpoint echoes Hume's sentimentalism); Parfit (fellow externalist about reasons but a realist -- Street's evolutionary argument challenges Parfit's non-naturalist realism); Enoch (principal realist opponent who responds with a "third-factor" response)
