# Thomson, J.J. (1929--2020)

- **Tradition**: Analytic ethics; applied ethics; moral metaphysics
- **Major Works**: "A Defense of Abortion" (1971), *The Realm of Rights* (1990), "Killing, Letting Die, and the Trolley Problem" (1976), *Normativity* (2008)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Moral particularism / pluralism: there is no single overarching moral principle; morality consists of a diverse collection of moral verdicts about particular kinds of action
  - The right is prior to the good: rights and interpersonal claims determine what is permissible; goodness does not ground rightness
  - Goodness is attributive: there is no property of being "good simpliciter"; something is good only relative to a kind (good knife, good person)
  - The right to life does not entail the right to use another person's body without consent; even if a fetus is a person, abortion may be morally permissible
  - Trolley problem variations reveal that moral judgments track deontological constraints (do not kill one to save five) and options (you may sometimes favor yourself)
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The violinist argument: you wake to find yourself connected to a famous violinist who needs your kidneys for nine months; you did not consent. Thomson argues you are not morally required to remain connected, showing that a right to life is not a right to the use of another's body. This argument transformed philosophical discourse on abortion
  - The argument for the priority of right over good: obligations and permissions are not derived from maximizing the good; they arise from interpersonal claims and constraints conceptually prior to goodness
  - The argument against consequentialism: trolley cases and other thought experiments reveal our moral judgments track constraints and options that consequentialism cannot accommodate
- **Relevance**: `phil-thought-experiment` (the violinist, trolley problem variations, the expanding child -- Thomson is a master of thought experiment methodology), `phil-argument-reconstruct` (argument by analogy and case-based reasoning), `phil-normativity` (the metaphysics of rights and normative structure), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (goodness as attributive, the concept of a right)
- **Related**: Foot (originator of the trolley problem; Thomson extensively explored and expanded the thought experiment); Nozick (shared the view that rights are side constraints rather than goals to be promoted); Scanlon (Thomson's priority of the right and contractualist framework); Anscombe (fellow critic of consequentialism who influentially challenged modern moral philosophy)
