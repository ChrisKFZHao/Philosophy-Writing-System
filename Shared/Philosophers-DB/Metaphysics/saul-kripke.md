# Kripke, Saul (1940--2022)
- **Tradition**: Analytic philosophy; modal logic; philosophy of language
- **Major Works**: *Naming and Necessity* (1980, lectures delivered 1970), *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language* (1982), "Outline of a Theory of Truth" (1975), "Semantical Considerations on Modal Logic" (1963)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Rigid designation: proper names designate the same object in every possible world where that object exists; names are not disguised definite descriptions
  - Causal-historical theory of reference: a name's reference is fixed by an initial baptism and transmitted through a causal chain of communication
  - A posteriori necessity: there are necessary truths knowable only through empirical investigation (e.g., "water is H2O," "Hesperus is Phosphorus")
  - Contingent a priori: there are truths knowable a priori that are nonetheless contingent (e.g., "the standard meter bar is one meter long")
  - Origin essentialism: an object has its origin essentially; the particular sperm and egg from which a person originated are essential to that person
  - Natural kind terms are rigid designators that refer to underlying kind essences discovered empirically
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The modal argument against descriptivism: if "Aristotle" meant "the teacher of Alexander," then "Aristotle was the teacher of Alexander" would be necessary -- but it is contingent. Therefore names are not synonymous with descriptions
  - The necessity of identity argument: if a=b, then necessarily a=b; this follows from Leibniz's Law combined with the necessity of self-identity
  - The Humphrey objection to counterpart theory: when we say "Humphrey might have won," we mean Humphrey himself, not some otherworldly counterpart; counterpart theory misrepresents modal discourse
- **Relevance**: `phil-modal-arg` (a posteriori necessity, rigid designation, the architecture of modal reasoning), `phil-essence-detect` (origin essentialism as a paradigm case of essence), `phil-natural-kind-analyze` (natural kind terms as rigid designators), `phil-conceivability-arg` (relationship between a priori, necessity, and conceivability), `phil-conceptual-analysis` (critique of descriptivist analyses of reference)
- **Related**: Lewis (counterpart theorist challenged by the Humphrey objection; modal realist alternative to Kripke's moderate actualism); Fine (rejects Kripke's modal account of essence in favor of essence as prior to modality); Putnam (independently developed semantic externalism and causal theory of reference for kind terms); Wittgenstein (Kripke's controversial "Kripkenstein" interpretation of rule-following)
