# Fine, Kit (b. 1946)
- **Tradition**: Analytic metaphysics; philosophical logic; philosophy of language
- **Major Works**: "Essence and Modality" (1994), "The Question of Realism" (2001), *Semantic Relationism* (2007), "Guide to Ground" (2012), *Vagueness: A Global Approach* (2020)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Essence as prior to modality: essence is not reducible to necessary properties; necessity is to be understood in terms of essence (the Finean reversal)
  - Non-modal essentialism: an object's essence consists of the properties that make it what it is, which may not include all its necessary properties (Socrates necessarily belongs to {Socrates}, but this is not part of Socrates's essence)
  - Metaphysical grounding: grounding is a non-causal, constitutive form of metaphysical explanation, distinct from supervenience and entailment; it is a strict partial order
  - The grounding criterion for realism: a domain is "real" if facts in that domain are not grounded in facts outside it; anti-realism about a domain is the view that its facts are grounded in something else
  - Constitutive vs. consequential essence: the constitutive essence is what an object is; consequential essence is what follows logically from the constitutive essence
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The singleton-Socrates argument against modal essentialism: necessarily, Socrates belongs to {Socrates}, but this is no part of what it is to be Socrates; therefore essence is not adequately captured by necessary predication
  - The Aristotle argument for the priority of essence: if one asks why Aristotle is necessarily a human, the answer invokes Aristotle's essence; this explanatory asymmetry shows that essence grounds necessity, not the reverse
  - The argument for grounding as primitive: grounding resists reduction to modal or counterfactual notions; attempts to define grounding in terms of necessitation plus other conditions fail to capture its explanatory character
