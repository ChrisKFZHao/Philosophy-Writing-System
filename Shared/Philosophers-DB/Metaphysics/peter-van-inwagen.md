# van Inwagen, Peter (b. 1942)
- **Tradition**: Analytic metaphysics; philosophy of religion; free will
- **Major Works**: *An Essay on Free Will* (1983), *Material Beings* (1990), *Metaphysics* (1993), *The Problem of Evil* (2006), *Existence: Essays in Ontology* (2014)
- **Key Positions**:
  - Incompatibilism about free will: free will is incompatible with determinism; the Consequence Argument shows that determinism entails that no one has a choice about anything
  - Organicist answer to the Special Composition Question: composition occurs exactly when the activity of a life constitutes an organism; there are no artifacts, no mereological sums, no inanimate composites
  - Quinean meta-ontology: ontological questions should be settled by reading existential quantifications off our best theories and assessing the theoretical cost
  - The Mind argument: indeterminism fares no better than determinism in securing free will because indeterministic events are matters of luck, not of agential control
  - Mereological nihilism about non-living things: there are only simples and living organisms; tables and chairs do not exist
- **Key Arguments**:
  - The Consequence Argument: if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of laws of nature and remote past events; we have no choice about the laws or the past; therefore we have no choice about our acts (formalized using a modal transfer-of-powerlessness operator N)
  - The argument for organicism: the only principled, non-arbitrary answer to "when do things compose something?" is "when they constitute a life"; all other answers generate vagueness and borderline cases
  - The argument from evil (logical form): the existence of gratuitous evil is logically incompatible with an all-powerful, all-knowing, wholly good God; van Inwagen defends the free-will defense against this argument
- **Relevance**: `phil-ontological-commitment` (meta-ontology and Quinean methodology for ontological questions), `phil-identity-persistence` (the Special Composition Question and its answers for persistence and composition), `phil-premise-detect` (the Consequence Argument's modal logic and premise structure), `phil-modal-arg` (transfer of powerlessness and necessity in the Consequence Argument)
- **Related**: Quine (methodological influence for van Inwagen's meta-ontology); Lewis (Lewis's unrestricted composition as the rival answer to the Special Composition Question); Plantinga (ally on incompatibilism; rival on the free-will defense); O'Connor (contemporary agent-causal libertarian engaging with van Inwagen's Mind argument)
